12.1 Instagram Posts and the Feed

An Instagram post is a stack of lines, from the account name at the top down to the time at the bottom, and the posts sit in the feed: the home screen of the app, with the top bar, the row of Stories circles, and posts one after another, all scrolling together. The module class is ig, and the feed adds ig-feed. Before we begin, here's a whole feed with every kind of post the workskin can do. The full HTML is underneath it, which you can copy and modify yourself. The rest of this section takes the HTML apart so that you can modify all the individual parts to say exactly what you want.

12.2 The Container

Every Instagram post in this chapter sits in a feed, the same way every iMessage sits in its chat window. The feed is an ig-feed app box with a theme, with an app-screen box inside it that holds everything that scrolls: the top bar, the Stories row and the posts, in that order. The top bar and the Stories row are optional, so leave them out if you only want the posts:

<div class="ig-feed app theme-auto">
  <div class="app-screen">
    <p class="header">the top bar</p>
    <div class="ig-stories">the Stories circles</div>
    <div class="mob ig post theme-auto">a post</div>
    <div class="mob ig post theme-auto">another post</div>
  </div>
</div>
Options: showing the whole feed, and light or dark

Once the feed is taller than a phone screen it scrolls, and the top bar and Stories scroll away with the posts, like in the app. To show the whole feed at once instead, add no-scroll: <div class="ig-feed app theme-auto no-scroll">.

theme-auto follows the reader's device. Swap it for theme-light or theme-dark to pick one yourself (see Light, Dark and Auto Themes).

12.2.1 Header

At the top of the feed go the top bar and the row of Stories circles. Both are optional.

The top bar is an header line, with the + on the left, the logo in the middle and the heart on the right. The logo is a picture, and the word typed inside it is for readers with the workskin off. The little red dot on the heart means there's something new, so leave out the badge span if there isn't:

Options: ring colours, your own circle, and pictures
  • With nothing added, the circle gets the colourful ring of a new story. Add close for the green ring of a Close Friends story, or plain for no ring at all.

  • Long names end in "...", so you can type the whole name.

12.6.10 Group Chats

For a group, put the group's name in the header, with two pictures in an ig-collab span (see Instagram DM Window). In the chat, Instagram puts each person's name above their first message and their picture beside their last one. To do that, wrap each run of messages from one person in a row: the picture first, then a who line with their name, then their messages. For a character with a profile, the picture and the name both come from it: a pfp span in the picture, and a handle span in the who line with the name typed inside it in a hide span. The picture always sits at the bottom of the run, however many messages there are:

RoseLandry:

the lasagne is still here if anyone wants it

RoseLandry: and the garlic bread. I made too much

The full HTML for this run of messages

    <span class="pfp pfp-rose"></span>
  </span>
  <p class="who">
    <span class="handle pfp-rose"><span class="hide">RoseLandry</span></span><span class="hide">:</span>
  </p>

The name shows above the first message, so that one only gets a hidden colon after the name. Every message after it in the run keeps the full hidden label, so readers with the workskin off still know who sent it. Your own messages don't go in a row, because Instagram doesn't show your own picture.

12.6.11 Reactions

Reactions are the little pill hanging off the bottom of a bubble. While everyone has reacted with the same emoji, the pill shows the emoji and their faces. As soon as someone uses a different emoji, it switches to the emojis and a number instead:

RoseLandry:

the lasagne is still here if anyone wants it

[Reactions: 😂 from Shane and Hayden HP]

Ilya: I already ate half of it

[Reactions: 💀❤️ 3]

The pill is a line of its own, a reacts paragraph straight after the message it belongs to. Add out under one of your own messages. For faces, put the emoji first and then an avatar span for each person, the same as anywhere else: a pfp span for a profile, or initials with a colour. The hidden "from" part tells readers with the workskin off who reacted:

<p class="msg in">the lasagne is still here if anyone wants it</p>
<p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>😂<span class="hide"> from Shane and Hayden </span><span class="avatar"><span class="pfp pfp-shane"></span></span><span class="avatar colour4">HP</span><span class="hide">]</span></p>

Instagram shows three faces at most, then the rest as a number, like +2. Type it straight after the last face. For the number version, type the emojis and the total:

<p class="reacts out"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>💀❤️ 3<span class="hide">]</span></p>
