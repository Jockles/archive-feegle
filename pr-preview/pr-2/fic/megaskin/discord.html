18.2 The Container

The first step is a container for the Discord window:

<div class="mob dc theme-auto"></div>

dc is the module class for Discord, and theme-auto shows it in Discord's dark or light mode to match the reader. Everything else in this chapter goes inside this container.

Options: light or dark

Swap theme-auto for theme-light or theme-dark to pick one yourself (see Light, Dark and Auto Themes).

Options: Discord at full width

Discord is the width of a phone like every other chat, but it's the one skin that's optimised for working full width too. Add wide to the container for Discord on a computer (see Phone Width or Full Width).

18.2.1 Header

18.2.1.1 Channel Header

For a channel header, use this:

# general: for general conversation (keep it clean!)

<div class="dc-header hashtag">
  <p class="dc-channel"><span class="hide"># </span>general<span class="dc-header-divider"></span><span class="hide">: </span><span class="dc-topic">for general conversation (keep it clean!)</span></p>
</div>

The header is a div with dc-header, and the channel name is a dc-channel paragraph inside it. Adding hashtag puts the # symbol before the channel name.

Options: locked channels, and no description
  • Use locked instead of hashtag for a locked channel - the icon is added for you.
  • You can leave the description out if you don't need it. Take out everything from dc-header-divider to the end of the dc-topic span.
How it works: the spans in the header

Inside the header, dc-header-divider is the thin line between the channel name and its description, and dc-topic is the grey description. The spans with hide hold text that only shows if a reader has the workskin switched off, like the # and the colon, so the header still makes sense without the styling.

18.2.1.2 Direct Message Header

For a DM, use at-symbol on the channel header, with the person's name, and put their name in the chat box too, like the direct message at the top of the chapter:

@ DropSomeKnowledge

<div class="dc-header at-symbol">
  <p class="dc-channel"><span class="hide">@ </span>DropSomeKnowledge</p>
</div>
Options: a DM on one character's account

If the DM is on one character's account, add pov- and their profile name to the container, like <div class="mob dc theme-auto pov-shane">, and anyone with a profile shows up under whatever nickname Shane has given them. Their pictures stay the same - Discord doesn't let you change someone else's (see Their Phone).

18.2.2 Footer

18.2.2.1 Chat Box

For the "Message #general" box at the bottom of the window, use this, with your own channel name in place of the text. It goes after all of the messages:

Message #general

<div class="footer">
  <p><span class="msgbar">Message #general</span></p>
</div>
18.2.2.2 Typing Indicator

To show that one or more people are typing, add this after the chat box:

SwankyUsername and DropSomeKnowledge are typing...

<p class="dc-typing typing-indicator"><span></span><span></span><span></span><b>SwankyUsername</b> and <b>DropSomeKnowledge</b> are typing...</p>

The three empty spans are the dots. Put the names of everyone who's typing after them.

18.2.3 Long Chats

Once a Discord chat gets long, the window stops growing at about the height of a phone screen and scrolls instead, so one long conversation doesn't take over the page - like the server channel at the top of the chapter. There's nothing to add - it just happens.

Options: showing the whole conversation

If you'd rather show the whole conversation at once, add no-scroll to the container: <div class="mob dc theme-auto no-scroll">. The window then grows as tall as it needs to. That's worth doing for a short burst of messages in the middle of a scene, so nobody misses a message tucked away in a scroll box (see Scrolling Long Chats).

How it works: what scrolls

Unlike the phone-style chats, the whole window scrolls, so the channel header and the chat box move with the messages rather than staying put.

18.3 Normal Messages

18.3.1 Simple Message

A Discord message needs three things: a name, a timestamp and the message itself. They all go inside a div with the class msg:

SwankyUsername 10/01/2022

Hello, world! Testing ordinary text.

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">10/01/2022</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">Hello, world! Testing ordinary text.</span></p>
</div>
Names that are too long

A long username goes onto a second line when it doesn't fit beside the time. Discord usernames can only be 32 characters long, so most fit at phone width. If one doesn't, and you'd rather it stayed on one line, use a shorter display name.

18.3.2 Message Timestamp

Each message has a timestamp next to the name, in a span with time. You can write anything in it: "10/01/2022", "Today at 12:00 PM" or "Yesterday at 1:12 AM".

18.3.3 Message Text

The message itself goes in dc-text. To write a message over several lines, give each line its own dc-text and put a line break between them:

SwankyUsername 10/01/2022

Robots will take over the world.
You will see.

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">10/01/2022</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">Robots will take over the <b>world</b>.</span><br> <span class="dc-text">You <i>will</i> see.</span></p>
</div>

You can use bold, italics and strikethrough inside the text as normal.

18.3.4 Reply to Message

To reply to a message, add a reply paragraph inside the message container, before the name line:

Replying to message by: SwankyUsername Robots will take over the world.

DropSomeKnowledge Yesterday at 9:40 AM

Fine, we will see.

[Reactions: 😡 12 😂 14]

<div class="msg">
  <p class="reply"><span class="hide">Replying to message by: </span> <b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="dc-text">Robots will take over the world.</span></p>
  <p><b class="dc-name colour6">DropSomeKnowledge</b> <span class="time">Yesterday at 9:40 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">Fine, we will see.</span></p>
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span><span class="dc-reaction">😡 <span class="count">12</span></span> <span class="dc-reaction me">😂 <span class="count">14</span></span><span class="hide">]</span></p>
</div>

It draws the curved line and the little preview of the message being replied to. Long previews get cut short with "..." automatically.

Options: matching the little picture

Give the name in it the same look as the original sender's, so the tiny profile picture matches: the same colour class, the same dc-pfp span with their uploaded picture in it, or, if they have a profile, the same dc-pfp span and profile name.

18.3.5 Edited Message

For the little "(edited)" tag at the end of a message, add an edited span inside the dc-text:

SwankyUsername 10/01/2022

ok that was dramatic of me (edited)

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">10/01/2022</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">ok that was dramatic of me<span class="edited"> (edited)</span></span></p>
</div>

18.3.6 Emoji Reactions

Reactions go on a line of their own under the message, a reacts paragraph. Put each one in a span with dc-reaction, with the emoji first and the count in count:

[Reactions: 😡 12 😂 14]

<p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span><span class="dc-reaction">😡 <span class="count">12</span></span> <span class="dc-reaction me">😂 <span class="count">14</span></span><span class="hide">]</span></p>

To add more than one reaction to a message, put them all in the same reacts line.

Options: your own reaction

Add the class me to a reaction if you want it to look like your point-of-view character reacted too, which gives it a blue outline.

How it works: the hide span, and screen readers

The hide spans put [Reactions: ] round the line for anyone reading with the workskin switched off, the same as in the other chats. Standard emojis are read out by screen readers, so they don't need a description.

18.3.7 Spoiler Text

To hide a word until the reader clicks it, wrap it in dc-spoiler-text:

DropSomeKnowledge Yesterday at 9:41 AM

also the robot is my cat on the keyboard, you know

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour6">DropSomeKnowledge</b> <span class="time">Yesterday at 9:41 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">also the robot is <span class="dc-spoiler-text">my cat on the keyboard</span>, you know</span></p>
</div>

18.3.8 User Mentions

For an @mention, wrap it in a span with dc-mention, inside a dc-text:

SwankyUsername Yesterday at 11:15 PM

hey @DropSomeKnowledge your cat is a genius

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">Yesterday at 11:15 PM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">hey <span class="dc-mention">@DropSomeKnowledge</span> your cat is a genius</span></p>
</div>
Options: mentioning your point-of-view character

If you want it to look like your point-of-view character is the one being mentioned, add the class me. The whole message is then highlighted with a gold bar down the side:

DropSomeKnowledge Yesterday at 11:20 PM

yo @ElDorado wake up, you're missing this

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour6">DropSomeKnowledge</b> <span class="time">Yesterday at 11:20 PM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">yo <span class="dc-mention me">@ElDorado</span> wake up, you're missing this</span></p>
</div>
Options: mentioning a role

You can mention a role too, like @losers, with dc-role-mention:

SwankyUsername Yesterday at 11:21 PM

hey @losers look at this

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">Yesterday at 11:21 PM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">hey <span class="dc-role-mention">@losers</span> look at this</span></p>
</div>

18.3.9 Blockquotes and Code Blocks

To make it look like a character has quoted someone, or sent some code, use these:

SwankyUsername Today at 9:12 AM

this is the best thing I've ever watched

print("you need to go outside")

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">Today at 9:12 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-quote"> <span class="dc-quote-line"></span> <span class="dc-text dc-quote-text">this is the best thing I've ever watched</span> </span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text dc-code">print("you need to go outside")</span></p>
</div>

18.3.10 Dividers

To show time passing, add a date divider between messages:

October 2, 2022

<div class="dc-divider">
  <p><span class="dc-divider-label">October 2, 2022</span></p>
</div>
Options: a NEW line

To split old messages from new ones, add a red line with a NEW tag:

New

<div class="dc-new-divider">
  <p><span class="dc-new-tag">New</span></p>
</div>
Options: a date and a NEW line together

If the date has changed and there are new messages, you can use both in one line:

October 3, 2022 - New

<div class="dc-new-divider">
  <p><span class="dc-divider-label">October 3, 2022</span><span class="hide"> - </span><span class="dc-new-tag">New</span></p>
</div>

18.3.11 New Member Announcement

To show that someone has joined the server, use a message with dc-new-user instead of the name and text:

Welcome, BugsPunny. We hope you brought pizza. Yesterday at 6:12 AM

<div class="msg">
  <p><span class="dc-new-user">Welcome, <b class="colour4">BugsPunny</b>. We hope you brought pizza.</span> <span class="time">Yesterday at 6:12 AM</span></p>
</div>

18.3.12 Bots

To give a user the BOT tag, add an empty span with the class dc-bot after their name:

BugsPunny [Verified Bot] Yesterday at 6:12 AM

beep boop. ALL HAIL OUR ROBOT OVERLORDS

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour4">BugsPunny</b> <span class="dc-bot"></span><span class="hide"> [Verified Bot] </span> <span class="time">Yesterday at 6:12 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">beep boop. ALL HAIL OUR ROBOT OVERLORDS</span></p>
</div>

18.4 Images and Links

18.4.1 Images and Emoji

To send a picture, put a photos one line in a message, with one photo inside it holding the image tag, the same as in every chat app. The web address of the image goes in src. Always fill in the alt text, so anyone using a screen reader knows what's in the picture:

SwankyUsername Today at 9:02 AM

my captain

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">SwankyUsername</b> <span class="time">Today at 9:02 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">my captain</span></p>
  <p class="photos one">
    <span class="photo"><img src="https://64.media.tumblr.com/c3c4ecb5bfed8fa9f0590c54206ddb30/cff2318529321620-44/s1280x1920/42befcc40a517e1f202dda40783be2ffb95c1d92.pnj" alt="Macklin Celebrini at the IHF Worlds, dressed in his red jersey and black helmet. He's surrounded by sparkles, a red vignette, and hearts containing the Canadian flag"></span>
  </p>
</div>
Options: custom emoji

Custom emoji work the same way, with the emoji's picture hosted somewhere else. dc-emoji is a big emoji sent on its own, like <p><img class="dc-emoji" src="..." alt="emoji name"></p>, and dc-inline-emoji is a small one that sits in a line of text.

18.4.2 Spoiler Image

You can hide an image the same way. Add dc-spoiler to the photo, and the word SPOILER is added on top for you:

DropSomeKnowledge Yesterday at 9:42 AM

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour6">DropSomeKnowledge</b> <span class="time">Yesterday at 9:42 AM</span></p>
  <p class="photos one">
    <span class="photo dc-spoiler"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat2.jpg" alt="Black and white cat screaming at the camera, on a red patterned rug"></span>
  </p>
</div>

18.4.3 Link Embeds

For the preview box Discord makes when someone posts a link, add a link-card after the line with the link in it. The small lines are link-desc and the title is link-title:

DropSomeKnowledge Today at 9:10 AM

this is the best thing I've ever watched https://youtube.com/watch?v=robots

YouTube

Options: real links, fake links and a preview picture

Use a real link with the <a> tag, or write it as ordinary text with the class fake-link to make it look like one. To add a preview picture, put it in a link-img paragraph at the end of the card.

18.5 Characters

The name goes in a bold tag with the class dc-name, and the round profile picture comes with it. There are three ways to do it: a plain coloured circle, a picture you upload, or a character profile (see Character Appearances and Profiles). Profiles are optional, and a busy server is full of people who only post once.

18.5.1 A Coloured Circle

Add one of colour1 to colour10 to the dc-name - it colours both the name and the round profile picture. Discord doesn't put initials in the circle, so this way gives a plain coloured circle:

mackattack Today at 9:12 AM

who has the aux at practice

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour1">mackattack</b> <span class="time">Today at 9:12 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">who has the aux at practice</span></p>
</div>

Give every character in your chat their own colour, and always use the same one for the same character.

Options: no colour, or your own colours
  • With no colour class, the name is plain and the circle is Discord blue.
  • The colours for colour1 to colour10 are set in the core CSS and shared with every app, so you can change them there.
How it works: light and dark name colours

These are the same ten colours as the picture circles in the other apps, with the names in their own light and dark shades so they're easy to read in either mode.

18.5.2 Uploading a Profile Pic

For an uploaded picture, put a dc-pfp span first inside the name, with the picture tag in a pfp span with alt text, and type the username after it. Add a colour class for the name colour:

samthecat Today at 9:16 AM

not me, I'm a cat

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name colour6"><span class="dc-pfp"><span class="pfp"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat2.jpg" alt="Profile picture of samthecat, a black and white cat screaming at the camera"></span></span>samthecat</b> <span class="time">Today at 9:16 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">not me, I'm a cat</span></p>
</div>
  • The username is typed, so it shows whether the workskin is on or off, with no hidden copy needed.
  • A wide picture needs class="landscape" on the picture tag, or it won't fill its circle.
Options: a picture in a reply

18.5.3 Using a Profile

To use a character's profile, put a dc-pfp span first inside the name, with their profile in it, the same as the other modules, and add their profile name to dc-name for their name colour. That gives them the picture and the name colour from their profile, so you can leave out the colour class:

shanehollander Today at 9:15 AM

some of us have practice at 10. please stop pinging the whole server

<div class="msg">
  <p><b class="dc-name pfp-shane"><span class="dc-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-shane"></span></span><span class="handle pfp-shane"><span class="hide">shanehollander</span></span></b> <span class="time">Today at 9:15 AM</span></p>
  <p><span class="dc-text">some of us have practice at 10. please stop pinging the whole server</span></p>
</div>
How it works: where the name comes from

The name can come from their profile as well - here it's Shane's Discord username, with the hide span so readers with the workskin off still know who's talking. Or just type their name in.

18.6 A Part on Its Own

You don't need a whole channel. Any part works by itself inside its container. Here's one message with no header and no message box:

mackattack Today at 9:12 AM

who has the aux at practice

<div class="mob dc theme-auto">
  <div class="msg">
    <p><b class="dc-name colour1">mackattack</b> <span class="time">Today at 9:12 AM</span></p>
    <p><span class="dc-text">who has the aux at practice</span></p>
  </div>
</div>

There's more on this in A Part on Its Own, in the Core chapter.
