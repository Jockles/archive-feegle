Before we begin, here's the two kinds of chat you can build: a message between two people, or a group chat. The full HTML is underneath each, which you can copy and modify yourself. The rest of the chapter takes the HTML apart so that you can modify all the individual parts to say exactly what you want.

Each part starts with the simplest version that works. If you want more, the Options boxes show the other things that part can do, and the How it works boxes explain what each bit of the code is for. You can skip both and still end up with a chat.

4.2 The Container

Every iMessage chat sits inside one box, called the container. It has a top bar with the contact's name, and then mob-body, which holds all of the messages. This is the smallest chat you can make:

Ilya

are you awake

<div class="mob imsg theme-auto">
  <p class="mob-header">Ilya</p>
  <div class="mob-body">
    <p class="msg in">are you awake</p>
  </div>
</div>

Copy that, change the name and the message, and you have a chat. Everything else in this chapter goes inside it.

Options: size, scrolling, and light or dark

These work the same way in every app, so they're explained in the core chapter:

  • The chat is the width of a phone. Add wide to the container to make it fill the page (see Phone Width or Full Width).
  • A long chat scrolls inside its box. Add no-scroll to the container to show the whole thing at once (see Scrolling Long Chats).
  • theme-auto follows the reader's device - a white chat in light mode, a black one in dark mode. Swap it for theme-light or theme-dark to pick one yourself (see Light, Dark and Auto Themes).
How it works: the three classes on the container
  • mob - makes the box: the phone shape, the rounded corners and the scrolling. Every chat app in the skin uses it.
  • imsg - says this box is iMessage, so the bubbles inside it are grey and blue and the right shape.
  • theme-auto - picks light or dark.

Real messages also get a hidden name label for readers with the workskin off, which is covered in Simple Messages.

The </div> at the very end closes the container, and the one before it closes mob-body. If a chat looks broken, a missing </div> is the first thing to check.

4.2.1 Header

The top bar is mob-header, with the contact's name in it:

Ilya

<p class="mob-header">Ilya</p>
Options: a second line under the name

A <span> after the name adds a smaller grey line under it, like "iMessage" or "Mobile":

Ilya - iMessage

<p class="mob-header">Ilya<span class="hide"> - </span><span>iMessage</span></p>

For a group chat, put the name of the group in the header, and use the span for the members or "iMessage group".

How it works: the hidden dash

<span class="hide"> - </span> is only for readers with the workskin off. Without it the two lines would run together as "IlyaiMessage". With it, they read "Ilya - iMessage".

Names that are too long

A long name at the top goes onto a second line when it doesn't fit. On an iPhone it ends in "…" instead, but AO3 won't let the skin do that for you, so view your chapter at phone width, and if the name doesn't fit, type it in yourself, cut short with "…".

4.2.2 Footer

Two things can go at the bottom of a chat: the message bar, and the typing dots. Both are optional.

4.2.2.1 The Message Bar

To finish off the screen with the "iMessage" box along the bottom, add a footer paragraph after the mob-body, still inside the chat. Keep it all on one line:

+ iMessage 🎤 

<p class="footer">
  <span class="plus">+</span><span class="hide"> </span>
  <span class="msgbar">iMessage<span class="hide"> </span><span class="mic ic ic-mic">&#127908;</span></span>
</p>
Options: a message that's halfway written

For a message someone is halfway through writing, add typed to the box so the words go black, swap "iMessage" for what they're typing, and swap the microphone for the blue send arrow, imsg-send. Long messages wrap onto more lines, the same as on the phone:

+ ok so about last night, I think we need to talk about what happened before your mum ↑ 

<p class="footer">
  <span class="plus">+</span><span class="hide"> </span>
  <span class="msgbar typed">ok so about last night, I think we need to talk about what happened before your mum<span class="hide"> </span><span class="imsg-send ic ic-imsg-send">&#8593;</span></span>
</p>

To show the flashing line after the last word, see A Flashing Cursor.

How it works: the message bar, piece by piece
  • <span class="plus">+</span> - the + button.
  • <span class="msgbar"> - the box, with the word "iMessage" in it.
  • <span class="mic ic ic-mic"> - the microphone. It's one of the skin's icons, which is what ic ic-mic means (see How They Work in the HTML).
  • The hide spans keep a space between them for readers with the workskin off, so it reads "+ iMessage 🎤" rather than running together.
4.2.2.2 Typing Dots

The three dots that show someone is typing are a message bubble with three empty spans in it, so they belong to whoever is typing. Add typing-indicator to a message with in for someone else:

Ilya is typing... 

<p class="msg in typing-indicator">
  <span class="hide"><em>Ilya</em> is typing...</span>
  <span></span>
  <span></span>
  <span></span>
</p>
Options: your own character typing, and typing in a group

Or out for whoever owns the phone, halfway through writing something - the dots go on the right, in a blue bubble:

Shane is typing... 

<p class="msg out typing-indicator">
  <span class="hide"><em>Shane</em> is typing...</span>
  <span></span>
  <span></span>
  <span></span>
</p>

In a group chat, put their name above it, the same as their messages:

Hayden

is typing... 

<p class="who">Hayden</p>
<p class="msg in typing-indicator">
  <span class="hide">is typing...</span>
  <span></span>
  <span></span>
  <span></span>
</p>
How it works: three empty spans

Each dot is an empty <span></span>, and the skin turns each one into a little circle. The hidden "is typing..." is for readers with the workskin switched off, who would otherwise see nothing at all.

4.3 Normal Messages

4.3.1 Simple Messages

Each message is a paragraph with the class msg, plus one more class that says who sent it. in is a message from the other person, which shows as a grey bubble on the left, and out is a message from your point-of-view character, which shows as a blue bubble on the right. Give every message its own <p>, one after another, inside mob-body:

Ilya: are you awake

Shane: no

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>are you awake</p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>no</p>
How it works: each part of a message, piece by piece
  • <p class="msg in"> - the bubble. msg makes it a bubble, and in or out says which side it sits on and what colour it is.
  • <span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span> - only for readers with the workskin off. In a one-to-one chat no names are shown anywhere, so this label is how those readers know who sent what.
  • are you awake - the message itself.

With the workskin off, this reads:

Ilya: are you awake

Shane: no

4.3.2 Date and Time Lines

For a centred line with the time or date, use mob-sys:

Today 11:48 PM

<p class="mob-sys">Today 11:48 PM</p>
Options: other notes in the middle of a chat

It works for anything that isn't a message - a timestamp, "Yesterday", or a note partway through the chat, like when someone joins the group:

Ilya added Wyatt to the conversation

<p class="mob-sys">Ilya added Wyatt to the conversation</p>

4.3.3 Tapback Reactions

When someone reacts to a message, iMessage shows a tapback - a little bubble on the top corner of the message, with a tail of two dots. Make the message a div instead of a paragraph, put a reacts line first with the emoji in it, and then the message text in a paragraph of its own:

[Ilya reacted 👎]

Shane: ok yes. go to sleep, you have a game tomorrow

<div class="msg out">
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Ilya reacted </span>👎<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>ok yes. go to sleep, you have a game tomorrow</p>
</div>
Options: your own character's reaction

reacts on its own is grey, for someone else's reaction, and reacts me is blue, for your point-of-view character's:

[Shane reacted 😂]

Ilya: liar

[Ilya reacted 👎]

Shane: ok yes. go to sleep, you have a game tomorrow

<div class="msg in">
  <p class="reacts me"><span class="hide">[Shane reacted </span>😂<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>liar</p>
</div>
<div class="msg out">
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Ilya reacted </span>👎<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>ok yes. go to sleep, you have a game tomorrow</p>
</div>
Options: more than one reaction on a message

When more than one person reacts to the same message, iMessage stacks the tapbacks on the corner, overlapping, with the first one at the front. Put the reacts lines one after another at the top of the message. The first one gets the tail, and each one after it tucks in behind, a bit further along the bubble:

Scott:

[Shane reacted 😂]

[Hayden reacted ‼️]

[Ryan reacted 👍]

reminder that nobody is letting Ilya run the drills again

[Ilya reacted ❤️]

[Scott reacted 👎]

Shane: the kids loved it though

<p class="who">Scott<span class="hide">:</span></p>
<div class="msg in">
  <p class="reacts me"><span class="hide">[Shane reacted </span>😂<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Hayden reacted </span>‼️<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Ryan reacted </span>👍<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p>reminder that nobody is letting Ilya run the drills again</p>
</div>
<div class="msg out">
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Ilya reacted </span>❤️<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p class="reacts"><span class="hide">[Scott reacted </span>👎<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>the kids loved it though</p>
</div>
  • Each person gets their own reacts line, with their own emoji and their own hide text saying who reacted. Up to four stack neatly.
  • If your point-of-view character reacted, put their reacts me line first, so the blue one is at the front, like it is on a real phone.
How it works: where the tapback sits, and the hidden words
  • Any emoji works, but each person can only react once, so it's one emoji per tapback.
  • It sits on the right-hand corner of a message from someone else, and the left-hand corner of your own, so you don't need to do anything to place it.
  • The two hide spans say who reacted, for readers with the workskin off: [Shane reacted goes before the emoji and ] goes after it. With the workskin off, the reaction is a line of its own above the message: [Ilya reacted 👎], then Shane: ok yes. go to sleep, you have a game tomorrow.

A message with three reactions reads like this with the workskin off:

Scott:

[Shane reacted 😂]

[Hayden reacted ‼️]

[Ryan reacted 👍]

reminder that nobody is letting Ilya run the drills again

[Ilya reacted ❤️]

[Scott reacted 👎]

Shane: the kids loved it though

4.3.4 Delivered and Read

iMessage shows a little status line under your last message. Add it with status, straight after the message it belongs to, and write "Delivered", "Read 11:52 PM" or whatever you like:

Shane: call me?

Read 11:52 PM

<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>call me?</p>
<p class="status">Read 11:52 PM</p>

4.3.5 Edited Messages

iMessage puts a small blue "Edited" under a message that's been edited. Add it with edited, straight after the message, just like the status line:

Shane: see you at 7

Edited

<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>see you at 7</p>
<p class="edited">Edited</p>
Options: an edited message from the other person

For a message from the other person, use imsg-edited-in instead, so it sits on the left:

Ilya: see you at 8

Edited

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>see you at 8</p>
<p class="imsg-edited-in">Edited</p>

4.3.6 Green Text Messages

When your point-of-view character's message goes as a normal text instead of an iMessage, it turns green - like Shane's, because the signal in the rink is too bad for iMessage. The same thing happens when they're messaging someone without an iPhone. Add sms to an out message to make it green:

Shane: can't check, the signal in this rink is terrible

Shane: these are going as texts. it's like 2009 in here

<p class="msg out sms"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>can't check, the signal in this rink is terrible</p>
<p class="msg out sms"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>these are going as texts. it's like 2009 in here</p>

Only sent messages turn green, the same as on a real phone. Messages from someone without an iPhone are grey like everyone else's, so they don't need anything extra.

4.4 Pictures

A picture is built the same way in every chat app. Add gallery to the message, then put a photos one line inside it, with one photo holding the picture tag. The corners are rounded for you. The picture has to be hosted somewhere else (see Pictures and Alt Text), and your link goes in src, with your description in alt:

Ilya:

<div class="msg in gallery">
  <p class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em></p>
  <p class="photos one">
    <span class="photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat4.jpg" alt="A cat curled up inside an open suitcase"></span>
  </p>
</div>
Options: describing a picture instead of showing it

If you'd rather describe the picture than show it, put an img-desc span in the photo instead, with the description in square brackets:

Ilya:

[A blurry photo of the same cat, now asleep on Ilya's hotel bed] 

<div class="msg in gallery">
  <p class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em></p>
  <p class="photos one">
    <span class="photo">
      <span class="img-desc">[A blurry photo of the same cat, now asleep on Ilya's hotel bed]</span>
    </span>
  </p>
</div>
How it works: the picture tag
  • src - the link to your picture. It has to be the picture itself, so the link should end in something like .jpg or .png.
  • alt - your description of the picture. Screen readers read it out, and it shows up in place of the picture if the link ever breaks.
  • gallery - shrinks the bubble down to a thin edge around the picture.
  • photos one - the box the pictures sit in, with rounded corners. Swap one for two or three to send that many pictures as a grid, or leave the word out for four.
  • photo - one picture. Use one for each picture you send.

4.5 Characters

iMessage doesn't show profile pictures, so the name is all anyone needs. There are two ways to do it: type the name, or have it come from a character's profile (see Character Profiles).

4.5.1 The Name in the Header

Write the name straight into the header. That's all a one-off contact needs:

Ilya

<p class="mob-header">Ilya</p>
Options: a name from a profile

Put a name span in its place, with the name typed inside it in a hide span for readers with the workskin off. The name then comes from the character's profile, so it's the same in every chat, and it changes to whatever your point-of-view character has them saved as (see Their Phone):

Ilya - iMessage 

<p class="mob-header">
  <span class="name pfp-ilya"><span class="hide">Ilya</span></span><span class="hide"> - </span>
  <span>iMessage</span>
</p>

4.5.2 Names in a Group Chat

In a group, put a who line above a message to show who sent it. Your own messages don't get one, the same as on a real phone, so they keep the hidden label instead:

Hayden:

day two of camp. who's awake

Scott:

unfortunately me

Shane: been up since 5. the cones are in my car

<p class="who">Hayden<span class="hide">:</span></p>
<p class="msg in">day two of camp. who's awake</p>
<p class="who">Scott<span class="hide">:</span></p>
<p class="msg in">unfortunately me</p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>been up since 5. the cones are in my car</p>
Options: a name from a profile

For someone who only turns up briefly, type their name in the who line, like Hayden and Scott above. For a character with a profile, put a name span in the who line instead, with their name typed inside it in a hide span, and the name comes from their profile:

Ilya:

I am bringing extra cones. many extra cones

<p class="who">
  <span class="name pfp-ilya"><span class="hide">Ilya</span></span><span class="hide">:</span>
</p>
<p class="msg in">I am bringing extra cones. many extra cones</p>

You can mix the two in the same group, so main characters come from their profiles and everyone else is typed.

How it works: the hidden colon

The who line shows whether the workskin is on or off, so it only needs a hidden colon after the name, to read "Hayden:" followed by the message.

4.6 A Part on Its Own

You don't need a whole chat. Any part works by itself inside its container. Here's a message bar with a half-typed message, and one bubble in a small box, using loose:

+ I miss 🎤 

<div class="mob imsg theme-auto">
  <p class="footer">
    <span class="plus">+</span><span class="hide"> </span>
    <span class="msgbar typed">I miss<span class="hide"> </span><span class="mic ic ic-mic">&#127908;</span></span>
  </p>
</div>

Ilya: are you awake

<div class="mob imsg loose theme-auto">
  <p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>are you awake</p>
</div>
