There are two kinds of email in this chapter: Gmail, and a plain email window that doesn't look like any particular brand. Each part starts with the simplest version that works. If you want more, the Options boxes show the other things that part can do, and the How it works boxes explain what each bit of the code is for.

16.1 Gmail Emails

This is for emails in the style of the Gmail app: a subject line, then each email in the thread with the sender, the date, who it was sent to and the message, with Reply buttons at the bottom.

Before we begin, here's a whole thread. The full HTML is underneath, which you can copy and modify yourself. The rest of this section takes the HTML apart so that you can modify all the individual parts to say exactly what you want.

16.1.2 The Container

An email thread is a container with the module class gm. The subject line, the emails and the buttons all go inside it:

[the subject line, emails and buttons go here]

<div class="mob gm theme-auto">
  <p>[the subject line, emails and buttons go here]</p>
</div>
Options: size, scrolling, and light or dark

These work the same way in every app, so they're explained in the core chapter:

  • The email is the width of a phone. Add wide to the container to make it fill the page (see Phone Width or Full Width).
  • A long thread scrolls inside its box. Add no-scroll to the container to show the whole thing at once (see Scrolling Long Chats).
  • theme-auto follows the reader's device - a white email in light mode, a dark grey one in dark mode. Swap it for theme-light or theme-dark to pick one yourself (see Light, Dark and Auto Themes).
How it works: why there are tables in here

The subject line, the email headers and the Reply buttons are made with <table> tags, because tables keep everything lined up neatly on AO3. You don't need to know how tables work to use them: copy the whole block and only change the words between the tags.

  • <tr> is a row, and each <td> inside it is one box in that row.
  • <tbody> just wraps the rows. AO3 adds it if you leave it out, so it's written in to match.
16.1.2.1 Header

The subject line at the top is a table with a gm-title cell for the subject and a gm-star cell for the star. A gm-label span after the subject adds the little grey label, like "Inbox" - you can leave it out if you like:

Sunday dinner Inbox ☆ 
<table>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td class="gm-title">Sunday dinner <span class="gm-label">Inbox</span></td>
      <td class="gm-star">
        <span class="ic ic-star">&#9734;</span>
      </td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>

If you're only showing the replies further down a thread, you can leave the subject line out completely.

16.1.2.2 Footer

For the Reply, Reply all and Forward buttons, use a gm-footer around a table with the class gm-actions. If the email only went to one person, leave out the "Reply all" button in the middle:

↩ Reply ↩↩ Reply all ↪ Forward 
<div class="gm-footer">
  <table class="gm-actions">
    <tbody>
      <tr>
        <td><span class="ic ic-reply">&#8617;</span> Reply</td>
        <td><span class="ic ic-reply-all">&#8617;&#8617;</span> Reply all</td>
        <td><span class="ic ic-forward">&#8618;</span> Forward</td>
      </tr>
    </tbody>
  </table>
</div>

The buttons go at the very end, after the last email in the thread.

16.1.3 Emails

Each email in a thread is a gm-msg, with two parts inside it: the header, which says who sent it and when, and the body, which is the message.

16.1.3.1 Sender Header

Each email starts with a header, which has two rows. The first row has the sender and the date, and the second row has the "to" line and the little reply and menu symbols:

SH Shane Hollander <shane@example.com> Jan 25, 9:20 AM
to Mom, Ilya ▾ ↩ ⋯ 
<table>
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td rowspan="2" class="gm-pfp-cell">
        <span class="gm-pfp colour5">SH</span>
      </td>
      <td class="gm-from">Shane Hollander <span class="gm-address">&lt;shane@example.com&gt;</span></td>
      <td class="gm-date">Jan 25, 9:20 AM</td>
    </tr>
    <tr>
      <td class="gm-to">to Mom, Ilya <span class="ic ic-dropdown">&#9662;</span></td>
      <td class="gm-btns">
        <span class="ic ic-reply">&#8617;</span>
        <span class="ic ic-more">&#8943;</span>
      </td>
    </tr>
  </tbody>
</table>
  • gm-pfp is the round picture. Without an image it shows initials, and colour1 to colour10 give it a colour. Give each person the same colour every time.
  • gm-from is the sender's name, in bold. The gm-address span after it adds their email address in smaller grey text.
  • gm-date is the date and time, on the right.
  • gm-to is the "to Mom, Ilya" line. Write the names of everyone it went to, with "me" for your point-of-view character - Shane sent this one, so he isn't in it.
  • gm-btns holds the reply arrow and the three dots on the right.
How it works: the picture across two rows

The round picture on the left stretches down across both rows with rowspan="2". That's why it's only written once, in the first row, and the second row starts with the "to" line.

16.1.3.2 Message Body

The text of the email goes in a gm-body, as ordinary paragraphs, and you can use bold, italics, links and line breaks as normal. The header and the body together make one email, wrapped in gm-msg, which also draws the thin line between one email and the next:

SH Shane Hollander <shane@example.com> Jan 25, 9:20 AM
to Mom, Ilya ▾ ↩ ⋯

Please do not bring a cake.

We'll see you at 6. I'm bringing the salad.

16.1.4 Characters

The round picture and the name in the header can be done three ways: initials in a coloured circle, a picture you upload, or a character profile (see Character Appearances and Profiles). Profiles are optional, and a one-off email - from a coach, a landlord, a bake sale - doesn't need one.

16.1.4.1 Initials in a Coloured Circle

Type the initials inside the gm-pfp, and add colour1 to colour10 to give the circle a colour. Give each person the same colour every time:

<td rowspan="2" class="gm-pfp-cell">
  <span class="gm-pfp colour5">SH</span>
</td>

The colours for colour1 to colour10 are set in the core CSS and shared with every app, so you can change them there.

Names that are too long

A long name or email address goes onto a second line when it doesn't fit. That's what the Gmail app does with a long address too, so there's nothing you need to do.

16.2 Generic Emails

For emails that aren't from Gmail - a work account, a school inbox, or just a plain email program - there's a simple email window. It has a dark title bar, the From, To and Subject lines, the message, and a Reply, Forward and Delete bar at the bottom. It's a good one to use if you don't want it to look like any particular brand. Here's one with everything in it:

Subject: Charity gala - forms to sign

To: shane@example.com
Cc: ilya@example.com

Hi Shane,

Please sign the forms before Friday, and send me a headshot that isn't the one where you're asleep on the plane.

Yuna Hollander

16.2.2 The Container

The window is a container with the module class mail. The title bar, the header lines, the body and the button bar all go inside it:

<div class="mob mail theme-auto">[title bar, header lines, body and button bar go here]</div>
16.2.2.1 Header

The dark title bar is a paragraph with mail-header. The little minimise and close marks go in a span first, so they sit on the right, and then the title:

_ X - Charity gala - forms to sign

<p class="mail-header"><span>_ X</span><span class="hide"> - </span>Charity gala - forms to sign</p>

You can change the title to anything, like the subject line. If it's too long for the window, it gets cut off rather than wrapping onto a second line.

16.2.2.2 Footer

To finish off the window, add the bar of buttons with mail-btns. Each button is a span:

Reply - Forward - Delete

<p class="mail-btns"><span>Reply</span><span class="hide"> - </span><span>Forward</span><span class="hide"> - </span><span>Delete</span></p>

16.2.3 The Email

16.2.3.1 Header Lines

For the From, To, Cc and Subject lines, use a paragraph for each one with mail-field, and put the label in <b> so all the labels line up:

From: yuna@example.com

Subject: Charity gala - forms to sign

<p class="mail-field"><b>From:</b> yuna@example.com</p>
<p class="mail-field"><b>Subject:</b> Charity gala - forms to sign</p>
Options: To and Cc in the same box

To put To and Cc in the same box, separate them with a line break:

To: shane@example.com
Cc: ilya@example.com

<p class="mail-field"><b>To:</b> shane@example.com<br><b>Cc:</b> ilya@example.com</p>
Options: an address from a profile

The From address here is Yuna's work email, which is a second account in her profile (acc-work), with the address typed inside in a hide span for readers with the workskin off:

From: Yuna@HollanderHockey.com

Subject: Charity gala - forms to sign

<p class="mail-field"><b>From:</b> <span class="email pfp-yuna acc-work"><span class="hide">Yuna@HollanderHockey.com</span></span></p>
<p class="mail-field"><b>Subject:</b> Charity gala - forms to sign</p>
16.2.3.2 Attachments

Attachments go in a header line, as spans with mail-attachment. Add doc for a document icon or img for a picture icon - with neither, you get a paperclip:

Attached: gala-forms.doc (9 KB) headshot.gif (70 KB) last-year.zip (5 MB)

<p class="mail-field"><b>Attached:</b> <span class="mail-attachment doc">gala-forms.doc (9 KB)</span> <span class="mail-attachment img">headshot.gif (70 KB)</span> <span class="mail-attachment">last-year.zip (5 MB)</span></p>
16.2.3.3 The Message

The message itself is your paragraphs inside a div with mail-body:

Hi Shane,

Please sign the forms before Friday, and send me a headshot that isn't the one where you're asleep on the plane.

Yuna Hollander
Hollander Hockey Management

<div class="mail-body">
  <p>Hi Shane,</p>
  <p>Please sign the forms before Friday, and send me a headshot that isn't the one where you're asleep on the plane.</p>
  <p class="mail-signature">Yuna Hollander<br>Hollander Hockey Management</p>
</div>

If the email is really long, the body stops growing and scrolls, so the window stays about the height of a phone.

16.2.3.4 Signature

For a signature under the message, use mail-signature as the last paragraph in the body. It's smaller and grey, with a thin line above it:

Yuna Hollander
Hollander Hockey Management

<p class="mail-signature">Yuna Hollander<br>Hollander Hockey Management</p>
