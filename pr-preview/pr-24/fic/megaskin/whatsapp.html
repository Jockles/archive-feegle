Before we begin, here's the two kinds of chat you can build: a chat between two people, or a group chat. The full HTML is underneath each, which you can copy and modify yourself. The rest of the chapter takes the HTML apart so that you can modify all the individual parts to say exactly what you want.

WhatsApp Window

A WhatsApp chat has the same three parts as iMessage - a container, a header and a body. The only difference is the module class, wa, so the container is <div class="mob wa theme-auto"> and everything else goes inside it. First comes the header, with the contact's name:

Lily - online

<p class="mob-head">Lily<span class="hide"> - </span><span>online</span></p>

The header is the green bar. The span underneath is the status line, like "online", "last seen today at 9:41" or "typing...", or the list of people in a group. After the header comes <div class="mob-body">, which holds all of the messages. There's a fancier header with a picture and icons too, which is in the group chat - see Group Header with Picture and Icons, further down.

Simple Messages

WhatsApp bubbles hold more than one line of text - each one can have a time, ticks and a quoted reply in it. So instead of a single <p>, each message is a <div> with paragraphs inside:

Lily: are you still up?

23:41

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Lily:</em> </span>are you still up?</p>
  <p class="wa-time">23:41</p>
</div>

Same as iMessage, msg in is a white bubble on the left and msg out is a green bubble on the right. The first paragraph is the message itself, and wa-time is the little grey time in the bottom right of the bubble. tail is optional - it adds the little pointy corner to the bubble, and it's meant for the first bubble in a run from one person.

Each part of a message, piece by piece
  • <div class="msg in tail"> - the bubble, with everything inside it.
  • <span class="hide"><em>Lily:</em> </span> - only for readers with the workskin off. It goes at the start of the first paragraph, straight before the message. With the workskin on, the colour and side of the bubble show who's talking. With it off, they don't - and in a one-to-one chat there's no name shown anywhere else - so this label is how those readers know who sent it.
  • are you still up? - the message itself.
  • <p class="wa-time">23:41</p> - the time, which shows either way.

With the workskin off, this reads:

Lily: are you still up?

23:41

Swap the name for whoever is talking. There's more on this in Accessibility, in the Core, Themes, and Extras chapter.

Date Pills

The little grey pill with the date on it uses the same mob-sys line as iMessage - in WhatsApp, the workskin turns it into a pill for you:

YESTERDAY

<p class="mob-sys">YESTERDAY</p>

Read Ticks

To show that a message has been read, add a double tick after the time and wrap it in wa-read:

Shane: unfortunately

23:42 ✓✓

<div class="msg out tail">
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>unfortunately</p>
  <p class="wa-time">23:42 <span class="wa-read">✓✓</span></p>
</div>

The ticks turn blue. For a message that hasn't been read yet, leave off the wa-read span and type the ticks as plain text, and they stay grey. One tick means the message was sent but hasn't reached the other person's phone, two grey ticks mean it got there but hasn't been read, and two blue ticks mean it's been read. Here are all three side by side:

Mom - last seen today at 10:01

Shane: Sent, but not delivered yet

10:03 ✓

Shane: Delivered, but unread

10:03 ✓✓

Shane: Read

10:03 ✓✓

Replying to a Message

To quote the message someone is replying to, add a paragraph with wa-quote at the top of the bubble, before the reply:

[Replying to Lily:
TELL ME EVERYTHING]

Shane: after snacks. I'm starving

14:06 ✓✓

<div class="msg out tail">
  <p class="wa-quote"><span class="hide">[Replying to </span><b>Lily</b><span class="hide">:</span><br>TELL ME EVERYTHING<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>after snacks. I'm starving</p>
  <p class="wa-time">14:06 <span class="wa-read">✓✓</span></p>
</div>

Put the name of the person being quoted in <b>, then a line break, then the words of the original message. The three hide spans are for readers with the workskin off: [Replying to before the name, a colon after it, and ] at the end, so the quote reads "[Replying to Lily: TELL ME EVERYTHING]" and nobody thinks Lily has just sent it again.

When someone replies to a message you sent, their bubble quotes your message, and WhatsApp labels it "You" in green. So the bubble is a received one (in), the name in the quote is You, and it takes the green colour class, colour8:

[Replying to You:
it's done. I'm alive]

Lily: so proud of you

14:07

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p class="wa-quote colour8"><span class="hide">[Replying to </span><b>You</b><span class="hide">:</span><br>it's done. I'm alive<span class="hide">]</span></p>
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Lily:</em> </span>so proud of you</p>
  <p class="wa-time">14:07</p>
</div>

23:43

[Reactions: ❤️]

Group Chats

In a group, add a who paragraph as the first line of a bubble to show who sent it. WhatsApp gives everyone their own name colour, so add one of colour1 to colour10 as well. Use the same number as the person's picture circle, so their name and picture match:

should I bring the kids?

09:13

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p class="who colour4">Hayden<span class="hide">:</span></p>
  <p>should I bring the kids?</p>
  <p class="wa-time">09:13</p>
</div>

Give each person the same colour class every time, so their name stays the same colour all the way through the chat.

Or add the person's profile name to the who line instead of a colour class, like <p class="who pfp-shane">, and their name colour comes from their profile, the same in every chat (see Character Profiles, in the Core, Themes, and Extras chapter).

The who name shows at the top of the bubble whether the workskin is on or off, so it doesn't need a hidden label - just the hidden colon after it (<span class="hide">:</span>), so it reads "Hayden:" followed by the message. Your own messages have no who line, so they keep the full hidden label.

Group Header with Picture and Icons

For the full group header - back arrow, picture, group name, list of members, and the call and menu icons - use a <table> with the class wa-bar instead of the usual header paragraph. A table is the most reliable way to keep all of that in one neat row on AO3:

←

D

 dinner 🎥 ⋮
Rose, Jane, Hayden, You 
<table class="wa-bar">
  <tbody>
    <tr>
      <td rowspan="2" class="wa-back">&#8592;</td>
      <td rowspan="2" class="wa-pfp-cell"><p class="wa-pfp colour5">D</p></td>
      <td class="wa-title">dinner</td>
      <td rowspan="2" class="wa-actions">&#127909; &#8942;</td>

    </tr>

The table has two rows. The first row has the back arrow (wa-back), the picture (wa-pfp-cell), the group name (wa-title) and the icons (wa-actions). The back arrow, picture and icons have rowspan="2", which stretches them down across both rows. The second row only has the member list (wa-sub), which sits under the name. If the name or the member list is too long, the workskin cuts it short with "..." for you.

The picture is the same wa-pfp circle as in the group messages, so colour1 to colour10 work here too. You can use this header for a one-to-one chat as well - put "online" or "last seen..." where the member list goes, or "typing..." to show the other person is typing.
