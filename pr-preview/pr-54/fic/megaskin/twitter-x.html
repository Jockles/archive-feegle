8.1.6 Name and Handle

The first line, tw-top, has the account's picture, name, handle and the time:

<p class="tw-top"><span class="tw-pfp colour6">SH</span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="tw-name">Scott Hunter <span class="tw-tick ic ic-verified">✔</span></span> <span class="tw-handle">@SHunter</span> <span class="tw-time">· 5m</span></p>
Each part of the name line, piece by piece
  • tw-name is the bold display name.
  • tw-tick inside the name adds the blue verified tick. Leave it out if the account isn't verified.
  • tw-handle is the grey @handle.
  • tw-time is the time, with a dot in front.

A tweet on its own, or the first tweet in a reply chain, is shown the way an opened tweet looks on Twitter/X, with the handle and time on a second line under the name. In a feed (see The X App Screen) and in replies, the name, handle and time all share one line. If they don't fit, the handle gets cut off first, and can disappear completely, then a really long name gets cut off too, tick and all - see Long names, below. The time always stays.

Long names

Any name can be too long for its line, on any kind of account. When it doesn't all fit, the handle shrinks first and can disappear completely, then the name is cut off at the edge, tick and all. A padlock and the time always stay. Put the name in a plain <span> inside tw-name and it ends in "..." like on Twitter/X. Without the span it still works, but it stops at the last whole word that fits instead:

call sheet says 5am. who decided this

A poll is a set of tw-opt options, with a tw-pollinfo line for the number of votes and the time left. While a poll is still open, each option is just a blue outline. Polls work in a tw-reply as well as a tweet, like Stan's:

Bar sizes and the winning option

The tw-bar becomes the grey bar. The percentage in tw-pct is just text, so you can type any number there, like 58% or 3.2%. The bar can't read that number, though, so each option also gets a bar size class that sets how long its bar is. These are the only bar sizes the skin comes with, so pick the one nearest to the percentage: p0, p10, p20, p25, p30, p33, p40, p50, p60, p67, p70, p75, p80, p90 or p100. If you want a bar of an exact length, the CSS chapter shows where these sizes are set and how to add your own. When the poll has finished, the winning option also gets win, which makes its bar blue and its words bold.

8.1.23 A Scrollable Thread

A tweet with lots of replies can go in a scrolling window, with a "Tweet your reply" bar that stays at the bottom, like the thread at the top of this chapter. More about scrolling threads

  • <span class="twdm-back"> - the back arrow. The arrow inside it is an ic ic-back icon, which swaps the typed arrow for a picture.
  • <span class="tw-pfp"> - the round picture, the same as a tweet's. It can hold initials (<span class="tw-pfp colour3">RD</span>), a picture tag inside a pfp span, or a profile, like Shane's here (see Character Appearances and Profiles).
  • <span class="twdm-name"> - the name, under the picture. For a group, this is the group's name.
  • <span class="twdm-calls"> - the video and call buttons, as two icons.

The name can also come from a profile, in a name span with a hidden copy typed inside it:

<span class="twdm-name"><span class="name pfp-zim"><span class="hide">Zim</span></span></span>

After the header comes <div class="mob-body">, which holds all of the messages.

8.2.4 Simple Messages

Messages are paragraphs with msg and either in or out. Sent messages are blue and received ones are grey:

Shane: saw your tweet

Ilya: it was meant to be funny

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>saw your tweet</p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>it was meant to be funny</p>
Each part of a message, piece by piece
  • <p class="msg in"> - the bubble. in is grey on the left, out is blue on the right.
  • <span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span> - only for readers with the workskin off. It's hidden in the chat, where the colours and sides show who's talking. With the workskin off, they don't, and a one-to-one DM has no names shown anywhere else, so this label is how those readers know who sent it.
  • saw your tweet - the message itself.

With the workskin off, this reads:

Shane: saw your tweet

Ilya: it was meant to be funny

Swap the name for whoever is talking. There's more on this in Accessibility.

8.2.5 Dates and Times

Twitter/X puts the date and time in a small grey line between messages, whenever there's been a gap. It's the core mob-sys line, the same as the "Today 9:41" lines in other apps:

Thu 17 Sep at 22:14

Shane: saw your tweet

<p class="mob-sys">Thu 17 Sep at 22:14</p>
<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>saw your tweet</p>

The same line works for anything else the app tells you in the middle of a chat, like "Your safety number with Shane has changed".

8.2.6 Seen and Sent

Under your last message, Twitter/X says "Sent", or "Seen" once the other person has read it. Add it with twdm-status, straight after the message:

Ilya: I am always funny

Seen

<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>I am always funny</p>
<p class="twdm-status">Seen</p>

8.2.7 Reactions

To show a reaction under a message, add a twdm-reacts paragraph straight after it. Add out to put it on the right, under one of your own messages. The two hide spans are only for readers with the workskin off, so the reaction reads "[Reactions: 😏 1]" instead of a stray emoji:

Shane: ok it was a little bit funny

[Reactions: 😏 1]

Ilya: I am always funny

[Reactions: 🙄 1]

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>ok it was a little bit funny</p>
<p class="twdm-reacts"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>&#128527; 1<span class="hide">]</span></p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>I am always funny</p>
<p class="twdm-reacts out"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>&#128580; 1<span class="hide">]</span></p>

8.2.8 Replies

When someone replies to a particular message, Twitter/X shows "Replied to" and the name in small grey writing, then the message they're replying to in an outlined bubble, then the reply. The first two get twdm-replied and twdm-quote, and all three get out for one of your own replies. The hidden brackets round the quoted message are for readers with the workskin off:

Replied to Shane

(it wasn't)

Ilya: you laughed. I heard you

<p class="twdm-replied out">Replied to Shane</p>
<p class="msg out twdm-quote"><span class="hide">(</span>it wasn't<span class="hide">)</span></p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>you laughed. I heard you</p>

For someone else's reply, use in instead of out on the bubbles, and leave out off the twdm-replied line. If the quoted message is long, cut it short with "..." the way the app does.

8.2.9 Pictures in DMs

A picture in a DM is a message with pic added. Put a picture tag inside it, or add text as well and describe the picture in words:

Shane:

Ilya: [A photo of Ilya pulling a face at the camera]

<p class="msg out pic text"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>[A photo of Ilya pulling a face at the camera]</p>

8.2.10 Voice Messages

A voice message is a message with voice, holding a play button in twdm-play, an empty twdm-wave span (the CSS draws the sound bars) and the length in twdm-dur:

Ilya: ▶0:02

<p class="msg out voice"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span><span class="twdm-play ic ic-play">&#9654;</span><span class="twdm-wave"></span><span class="twdm-dur">0:02</span></p>

8.2.11 The Message Bar

To finish off the window, add the message bar after the body, inside the container. It's built the same way as iMessage's: the round twdm-plus button, then the twdm-field box with the word in it and the microphone at the end. The hide spans keep a space between the parts for readers with the workskin off:

+ Message 🎤

<p class="twdm-input"><span class="twdm-plus">+</span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="twdm-field">Message<span class="hide"> </span><span class="ic ic-mic">&#127908;</span></span></p>
A half-written message

For a half-written message, add typed to the twdm-field, write the message instead of "Message", and add the flashing cursor from the core if you like:

+ I did not agree| 🎤

<p class="twdm-input"><span class="twdm-plus">+</span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="twdm-field typed">I did not agree<span class="cursor">|</span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="ic ic-mic">&#127908;</span></span></p>

8.2.12 The App Buttons

The row of buttons along the very bottom of the app is optional. Put it last, after the message bar. Each button is an icon, and on gives the chat button its grey box, because that's the part of the app you're in:

⌂ 🔍 ⊘ 🔔 (3) 💬

<p class="tw-nav"><span class="ic ic-x-home">&#8962;</span> <span class="ic ic-x-search">&#128269;</span> <span class="ic ic-x-grok">&#8856;</span> <span class="tw-badged"><span class="ic ic-x-bell">&#128276;</span><span class="tw-badge"><span class="hide"> (</span>3<span class="hide">)</span></span></span> <span class="on ic ic-x-chat">&#128172;</span></p>
Notification numbers

For new notifications, put the bell and a tw-badge with the number in it together in a tw-badged span, as above. The blue circle stretches into a lozenge for longer numbers. The hidden brackets are for readers with the workskin off:

⌂ 🔍 ⊘ 🔔 (1,284) 💬

<span class="tw-badged"><span class="ic ic-x-bell">&#128276;</span><span class="tw-badge"><span class="hide"> (</span>1,284<span class="hide">)</span></span></span>

The header, the message bar and these buttons stay where they are, and once the chat gets long, the messages in between scroll.

8.2.13 Group Chats

For a group DM, put the group's name and picture in the header. Then each run of messages from one person goes in a twdm-row, with their round tw-pfp picture first, then their name in a who line, then their messages. The picture sits beside the last message of the run, the way it does in the app:

HP

Hayden:

are we still going out?

Hayden: Jackie says I'm allowed

<div class="twdm-row">
  <p class="tw-pfp colour4">HP</p>
  <p class="who">Hayden<span class="hide">:</span></p>
  <p class="msg in">are we still going out?</p>
  <p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Hayden:</em> </span>Jackie says I'm allowed</p>
</div>
Names, pictures and reactions in a group

The name shows either way, so the first message only needs a hidden colon after the name, not the full label - the messages after it, and your own messages with no name above them, still need that. The picture can be initials with a colour (colour1 to colour10), a picture tag inside a pfp span, or a profile, like Shane's:

Shane:

yes. Ilya is paying

<div class="twdm-row">
  <p class="tw-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-shane"></span></p>
  <p class="who">Shane<span class="hide">:</span></p>
  <p class="msg in">yes. Ilya is paying</p>
</div>

For a character with a profile, the who line can use their profile name too, in a name span with the name typed inside it in a hide span:

Zim:

I'm driving, so yes

<div class="twdm-row">
  <p class="tw-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-zim"></span></p>
  <p class="who"><span class="name pfp-zim"><span class="hide">Zim</span></span><span class="hide">:</span></p>
  <p class="msg in">I'm driving, so yes</p>
</div>

A reaction can have more than one emoji in it, and it goes inside the row, after the message it belongs to.
