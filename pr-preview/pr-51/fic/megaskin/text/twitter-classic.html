This chapter is Twitter as it was before it became X, in two separate modules: twc16 for hearts and round pictures from 2016 to 2019, and twc12 for the stars and square pictures of 2012 to 2015. Each one works on its own, so add whichever fits when your fic is set, or both if it covers both. Neither needs the Twitter/X module. The tweets use the same HTML as the Twitter/X chapter, so a tweet can move between any of the three by changing its container.

Building a Classic Tweet

The Tweet

A classic tweet is a container with mob twc16 (or mob twc12) and a theme. Inside, it's built exactly like a Twitter/X tweet: the name line in tw-top, the words in tw-text, and the buttons in tw-stats. The picture sits in a column of its own on the left, and everything else lines up beside it, the way Twitter looked back then:

Zim @ZimBits · 3h ⌄

Rookie camp is officially open and someone has already lost a skate

↩ 14 replies

⇄ 52 retweets

♡ 310 likes ( 311 once liked)

⇧

The name line works the same as in Twitter/X: the picture in tw-pfp can be initials with a colour, a picture tag, or a profile, and the name and handle can be typed or come from a profile. A verified tick goes inside the tw-name, as <span class="ic ic-verified">&#10004;</span>. Type the dot before the time yourself.

The Buttons

The row along the bottom is reply, retweet, like and share, with the number after each icon. It's the same as the Twitter/X row without the views and bookmark, which Twitter didn't have yet. The like is clickable: readers can tap it to like the tweet and tap it again to take it back, just like in the Twitter/X module. For a like or a retweet the account has already made, add tw-liked or tw-rt to that button's tw-stat, which makes it red or green:

<p class="tw-stat tw-rt"><span class="ic ic-tw-repost">&#8644;</span> 52<span class="hide"> retweets</span></p>

Retweets

For a tweet that someone else retweeted into the timeline, add a tw-label line at the top, before the name line:

⇄ Shane Hollander Retweeted

Zim @ZimBits · 3h

Rookie camp is officially open

<p class="tw-label"><span class="ic ic-tw-repost">&#8644;</span> Shane Hollander Retweeted</p>
<p class="tw-top"><span class="tw-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-zim"></span></span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="tw-name"><span class="name pfp-zim"><span class="hide">Zim</span></span></span> <span class="tw-handle"><span class="handle pfp-zim"><span class="hide">@ZimBits</span></span></span> <span class="tw-time">· 3h</span></p>
<p class="tw-text">Rookie camp is officially open</p>

Replies can start with <p class="tw-replying">Replying to <span class="tw-link">@ZimBits</span></p> after the name line, and pictures go in tw-media, the same as Twitter/X, one picture per tweet.

The Little Arrow

From 2016, every tweet had a small grey arrow in its top right corner, for the menu. Add it at the end of the name line, after the time: <span class="tw-more ic ic-dropdown">&#8964;</span>. Leave it off for 2012-15, when the time sat in that corner instead.

Likes in the Timeline

In 2018 Twitter started showing tweets your friends had liked. It's the same tw-label as a retweet, with a heart instead: <p class="tw-label"><span class="ic ic-tw-like">&#9825;</span> Rose Landry liked</p>.

Quote Tweets

A quoted tweet goes in a box under the words, in a tw-quote. Inside it, the name line has just the name and handle, with no picture, and then the quoted words:

Conversations

In a timeline, a tweet and a reply to it were joined by a grey line between their pictures. Add link to the first tweet's container, <div class="mob twc16 link">, and the line runs down from its picture to the picture of the tweet underneath. Chain as many as you like by adding link to each one except the last. You can see this in the timeline at the top of the chapter.

When you open a reply, the tweet it answers sits above it in timeline size, joined by the same line, and the reply itself is a big tweet with its "Replying to" line under the name:

An Opened Tweet

Add big to the container for a tweet that's been tapped on and opened. The handle moves under the name, the words get bigger, and the time is left out of the name line. The date line can say what the tweet was sent from, the way Twitter did: "11:04 PM · 10/20/18 · Twitter for iPhone". Under the words go the date in tw-date and the counts in tw-counts, with each number in bold, and the buttons have no numbers of their own. To finish it off, the reply box goes last, in twc-foot:

The Timeline

To show tweets the way they looked in the app, put them inside a twc16-feed (or a twc12-feed for twc12 tweets). The tweets inside lose their own borders and sit one under the other with a line between them, and the whole timeline scrolls once it's taller than a phone screen. The theme goes on the feed, so you don't need it on each tweet.

The bar along the top, twc-bar, is optional. On the home timeline it has the pov character's own picture in tw-me on the left and the page name in twc-title. For an opened tweet, swap the picture for a back arrow, and add a search button on the right if you like:

<p class="twc-bar"><span class="tw-me"><span class="pfp pfp-ilya"></span></span><span class="twc-title">Home</span></p>
<p class="twc-bar"><span class="ic ic-back">&#8592;</span><span class="twc-title">Tweet</span><span class="ic ic-x-search">&#128269;</span></p>

2012 to 2015

For Twitter before hearts, use twc12 instead of twc16, and twc12-feed instead of twc16-feed. Everything else in this chapter works the same way, apart from these differences:

  • the pictures are squares with slightly rounded corners
  • the like button is a star, which turns gold instead of red. Call them favorites in the hidden text and the counts, the way Twitter did
  • the reply button is a curved arrow, and share is three dots for "more"
  • the time sits on the right-hand side of the name line. Leave the dot off: <span class="tw-time">33m</span>
  • there's no little arrow in the corner, because that's where the time goes
  • an opened tweet's words are in a serif font, with the counts in capitals
  • the bar along the top is Twitter blue, with white writing and buttons

Timeline tweets on the website had a small grey "Details" link under them instead of buttons. To copy that, leave out the tw-stats and add <p class="tw-details">Details</p> instead. Here's one of each:

Themes

theme-dark is Twitter's night mode from 2019, a dark blue rather than black, and theme-auto switches to it when the reader's device is in dark mode, the same as every other app. Twitter didn't have a night mode in 2012-15, so for a twc12 fic, you might want theme-light - but it's your fic (or not I'm not your dad).
