The buttons and ticks in every app - the mic in the message box, the hearts and reblog arrows, the blue ticks and so on - are little pictures. This chapter shows how they work, how to take them out if you'd rather not use them, and what shows up instead if you do.

Where the Icons Come From

The pictures are hosted on squidge.org. Light chats get black icons and dark chats get white ones, and they follow your theme- class like everything else. A few are always the same colour, whatever the theme:

  • WhatsApp's blue read ticks
  • the blue verified badges on Twitter/X and Instagram
  • Instagram's red liked heart
  • the round send and mic buttons in iMessage and WhatsApp

Every icon the skin uses is in this album on squidge.org, and you're welcome to use them in your own skins or anywhere else.

If squidge is ever down, the icons won't show, and because the skin hides the symbols underneath them, those buttons will just be blank until it's back.

How They Work in the HTML

An icon is a span with ic and the icon's name, with a typed symbol still inside it:

<span class="tb-icon ic ic-heart">&#9825;</span>

Keep the app's own class (tb-icon here) as well - that's what sizes and spaces it. The symbol inside is the stand-in: it's what readers see if they have creator's style turned off, or if you take the icons out of your skin. Every example in this tutorial already has them, so if you copy your HTML from here, there's nothing else to do.

What Shows Up Without the Icons

Take the icons out and each one goes back to the symbol typed inside it:

  • iMessage: the mic is 🎤 and the send arrow is ↑ in the blue circle.
  • WhatsApp: the back arrow is ←, the video and menu buttons are 🎥 and ⋮, the message box has ☺, 📎 and 📷, with 🎤 or ➤ in the green circle, the read ticks are ✓✓ in blue, and link previews get 🔗.
  • Twitter/X: the verified badge is a blue ✔, and the voice note play button is ▶.
  • Instagram: the three dots are ⋯, the verified badge is a blue ✓, likes are ♡ (and a red ♥ when clicked), reposts are ↻ and shares are ➤. The comment bubble and bookmark go back to being drawn with CSS.
  • Tumblr: share is ⇧, reply is ✉, reblog is ⇄ and like is ♡. The bookmark goes back to being drawn with CSS.
  • Email: Gmail's star is ☆, reply is ↩, reply all is ↩↩, forward is ↪, the three dots are ⋯ and the little arrow by "to me" is ▾. Attachments in the email window get 📎, 🗺 for documents or 🖼 for pictures.

The Tumblr checkmarks and Discord's icons aren't part of this - they always use their own pictures.

With the Workskin Off

Readers with creator's style turned off don't get any of the pictures, but they do get the typed symbols, sitting in the text as plain characters. So the bottom of a Tumblr post reads "171 notes ⇧ ✉ ⇄ ♡", a WhatsApp message box reads "☺ Message 📎 📷 🎤", and an email header ends "to me, Ilya ▾ ↩ ⋯".

That's fine, and you don't need to describe every one. By the time a reader gets to the buttons they already know it's a Tumblr post or a WhatsApp chat (see Accessibility), so the buttons are just the furniture of the app. What matters is the salient stuff, and that's already in the text or in the hide spans:

  • Counts: Instagram's buttons read "♡ 2,431 likes", "↻ 12 reposts" and "➤ 7 shares", and a Tumblr post has its note count.
  • Ticks: WhatsApp's read ticks show as ✓✓ after the time, and verified badges as a ✓ after the name.
  • Buttons with words: Gmail's buttons still say "Reply", "Reply all" and "Forward", and Instagram's save button gets a hidden "Save".

If a button doesn't add anything to your scene, you can always leave it out of your copy of the HTML.

The CSS

Each app module ends with its own Icons section, after the auto theme. It starts with this, which turns each ic span into a square the size of the text around it:

#workskin .tb .ic {
  display: inline-block;
  width: 1em;
  height: 1em;
  overflow: hidden;
  text-indent: -100em;
  text-align: left;
  white-space: nowrap;
  vertical-align: -0.15em;
  background-repeat: no-repeat;
  background-position: center;
  background-size: contain;
}

text-indent: -100em pushes the typed symbol a long way off to the left, and overflow: hidden cuts it off, so all that's left is the picture, which background-size: contain fits into the square.

Then there's a line for each picture, in black for light chats, white for theme-dark, and white again inside @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark) for theme-auto:

#workskin .tb .ic.ic-heart { background-image: url(https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/15/heart-black.png); }
#workskin .tb.theme-dark .ic.ic-heart { background-image: url(https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/15/heart-white.png); }

A few apps have small fixes on top:

  • The round buttons: iMessage's send arrow and WhatsApp's mic and send buttons are whole pictures, so they fill the circle.
  • WhatsApp's message box: the icons sit in table cells, so they get a set width.
  • WhatsApp's top bar: it's coloured in every theme, so its icons are always white.
  • Twitter/X voice notes: one you sent sits in a blue bubble, so its play button is always white.
  • Instagram and Tumblr bookmarks: these are pictures in place of the CSS drawings earlier in their modules.
  • Link previews and attachments: these symbols come from the CSS rather than the HTML, so the picture goes in the same place.

Taking the Icons Out

To go back to the typed symbols for a whole app, delete its Icons section: everything from its Icons heading (like ---------- Tumblr: Icons ----------) to the end of the module. That's the only place they're set, so nothing else breaks.

To take out just one icon, take the ic classes off that span in your HTML, so <span class="tb-icon ic ic-heart"> becomes <span class="tb-icon">. Deleting only that icon's lines in the CSS won't do it, because the first rule in the section still hides the symbol, and you'll be left with a blank space.
