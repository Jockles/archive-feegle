Lesson 6. Belonging: clitics and inalienable nouns

Rule 6.1 [NEW] Short pronoun endings, called clitics, attach to the end of nouns, verbs and prepositions. One set does three jobs: it shows the owner of a noun, the object of a verb, and the person after a preposition. This is the Berber pattern of pronoun suffixes.

2sgte=kyou, your

Rule 6.2 [ESTABLISHED + Jockles] The prepositions are a "in", ed "with", gor "at, by", keik "about" and nel "from, of, given by".

Rule 6.3 [NEW] Belonging that cannot be cut loose. Kin, body parts and names are inalienable. They are never spoken bare unless someone is speaking generally: you say amu=i "my mother", adar=k "your foot", nu=t "his name". A bare amu means "a mother, mothers in general". This is a strong Berber feature and it suits a culture where family is taken from you and belonging is not a thing you assume.

Rule 6.4 [CANON + ESTABLISHED] The name phrase ek masa nu ... "I am called ..." remains valid as written. The dictionary also attests possessive pronouns: em "my, mine" and ema/eme/emi/emu "his, xir, their, her". The clitics in this lesson are a proposal that would sit beside these, and which set to use for possession is undecided (see Decisions for you).
