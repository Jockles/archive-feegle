Lesson 10. Numbers and counting

Rule 10.1 [SRD] The numbers 1 to 10 are single syllables: tsi 1 [ESTABLISHED], top 2 [ESTABLISHED], sor 3, kuz 4, fus 5 (from afus "hand"), dis 6, zas 7, tum 8, tiz 9, rew 10 [SRD, decided 30 September 2026].

Rule 10.2 [DECIDED] rew is the free form of ten; -rel is its bound form in compounds. N-rel means N tens: top-rel 20, sorel 30, kuz-rel 40, dis-rel 60, zas-rel 70, tum-rel 80, tiz-rel 90. sorel is a lexical fusion of sor + rel, written with a single r (the one exception to Rule 1.4).

Rule 10.3 [NEW] The number follows the noun. For 2 and above the noun is plural: riivken top "two ships". For 1 the noun is singular: riiv tsi "one ship".

Rule 10.4 [SRD] sedenuu means "to count".

Rule 10.5 [CANON] efin 50 and dekka 100 are irregular and stay as they are; the regular forms fus-rel and rew-rel are not used. 1000 is rekkat [SRD], an irregular word that is not built from dekka or -rel, in the way English "thousand" is separate from "hundred".

Rule 10.6 [DECIDED] Larger counts use thirties: N-sorel (top-sorel 60, sor-sorel 90, kuz-sorel 120, fus-sorel 150). Ages, days and close kin (up to thirty people) are counted in thirties; ordinary goods are counted in tens. 60 and 90 have both forms (top-sorel or dis-rel; sor-sorel or tiz-rel).

Rule 10.7 [DECIDED] The joiner -e- "and" puts the larger number first: efin-e-tsi 51, rew-e-tsi 11, sorel-e-tsi 31.

Rule 10.8 [DECIDED] Zero is kolak, the word for nothing or empty. Kolak is also used for zero in sums and counts.

Rule 10.9 [DECIDED] To say what place something has in a line (first, second, third), add -an to the number: soran third, kuzan fourth, fusan fifth, sorelan thirtieth. Two are special: toba means second and ebdan means first (from ebduu, to start). The last one is ereiban.

Rule 10.10 [DECIDED] To say how many times, put toba after the number: tsi toba once, top toba twice, sor toba three times.

Rule 10.11 [DECIDED] Fractions: xe is a half and xexe is a quarter (half of a half). A third has its own word, sorxe, which is still provisional. Every other fraction is tsi plus the order word: tsi fusan a fifth, tsi disan a sixth.

Rule 10.12 [DECIDED] The year is ar-ewel, "the great turn" (ar- great + ewel a turn, from eweluu). It runs from one marokkepu, the day all three moons are full, to the next, about ten moon-rounds. Published Tatooine sources name no seasons, so the stretches are our own assumptions: araplu is the great drought, the long dry stretch; arail is the great mist, the humid build-up before the new year; arumak is the mighty storm, the sandstorm stretch after it. Each is ar- plus a shortened root (aplu from aplutu, ail from aila, umak from umakkar). The lengths are six, three and one moon-rounds (confirmed): araplu six, arail three, arumak one, ten in all. The old word asagas is retired.

Rule 10.13 [DECIDED] The month is sorlev, one round of the moons, about thirty days. It is the rounded-off form of sorel-lev (thirty moon) and is learned as one word. Ten sorlev make an ar-ewel. khepi means time or hour, and asagi means season, the general word for araplu, arail and arumak.

Rule 10.14 [DECIDED] A stretch of ten days is usanna, three to a sorlev. The day is split into morning, tagasu, and evening, tamettu; dawn and dusk stay haro, noon stays khepatag and night stays nittu. These three words are new and were shaped on Berber and Tuareg ideas without copying them: usanna from a Berber word for days, tagasu from a Tamasheq morning greeting, tamettu from a Kabyle word for evening.

Rule 10.15 [DECIDED] Days relative to now are made by adding a verb time ending to kashka (day): kashkalu today, kashkanao yesterday, kashkaker tomorrow. The same ending works for the far times: kashkabor is the day after we are free, and kashkaqel is once upon a time, the story-day.
