The funeral was sombre. Ilya stood beside Ryan, him and Scott bracketing him closely. Shane... Shane had wanted to come. He'd said to Ryan that he would have come if he hadn't made other commitments during the bye-week other than All Star, that he wanted to be there for Ryan as a friend, even though they barely knew each other.

Ilya knew what was really happening. Shane was so scared that missing such a big promotional moment for himself when he was so stressed on the team was what actually kept him away. Ilya tried to understand, he did, but it seemed strange to think so much of hockey right now.

Ryan though... Ryan was struggling. More than Ilya had suspected. He carried himself like he was in pain constantly, and there was something in his front pocket that he kept grabbing hold of reflexively. It wasn't his phone, that was also in his hand almost constantly, and he kept looking at it like he was expecting to hear from someone, but it never lit up that Ilya saw.

When the ceremony was over, the three hockey players left the wake after barely ten minutes, heading to the Tim Horton’s next door instead. They were in the middle of nowhere, and Scott and Ilya had carpooled together after flying in to Toronto, so there was really no reason for them to stay. It was three hours back into the city. Both of them had flights the following morning — Scott to St. Louis where he was going to cheer the second half of the All Star competitions, and Ilya to Montreal, where he was going to wait for Shane and spend as much of their shared week off together as possible.

Sitting in the Tim Horton’s together with Ryan, they all stared at their coffees, eating the box of Timbits in the middle of the table mechanically.

Scott Hunter ✔ @HunterScott · 2m

Coming out is not the end of the story.

I am grateful every day to my team, who have treated me with kindness and support. Leading them to a Stanley cup probably helped, but they have been nothing but supportive of me since our win, and my public coming out.

Unfortunately, there are many players across this sport who do not receive that support. From their family, their friends, their team, their coaching staff, and their agents. There are dozens of people required to support a player at every level, and they must all be pulling you, the player, in the same direction.

I’ve been in those locker rooms. I’ve sat down with those coaches. The ones that don’t even realise what they’re saying when they talk down about men who might love other men. And the ones who do, who use that word as a serious insult across the ice.

So I call upon every player on every team to stand against this. Because being like this can only ever make your game worse. It keeps good players from reaching the top of their game. It stops talented kids from attending their camps. It makes teammates unable to trust each other on the ice.

So I call on every team, to pledge an end to this kind of locker room talk. I call on every captain to stop it on your ice. From my fellow MLH captains to the peewee coaches. We can help each other to play better hockey, and to be better friends.

There are teams out there — at all levels of play — that may not have supported me. That might have made the last year hell. I know there are parts of the MLH that still don’t like what I am, and what I stand for.

Thank you to the teams that have reached out and already pledged. You are making the sport better for everyone.

We, the players, are the face of this organisation.

We can change the game for everyone if we want.
