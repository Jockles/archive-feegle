6.2 The Container

A WhatsApp chat has the same three parts as iMessage - a container, a header and a body. The only difference is the module class, wa, so the container is <div class="mob wa theme-auto"> and everything else goes inside it. The top of the chat is the header, the middle is the background with the messages on it, and the bottom can have the message bar and the typing dots.

Options: size, scrolling, and light or dark

These work the same way in every app, so they're explained in the core chapter:

  • The chat is the width of a phone. Add wide to the container to make it fill the page (see Phone Width or Full Width).
  • A long chat scrolls inside its box. Add no-scroll to the container to show the whole thing at once (see Scrolling Long Chats).
  • theme-auto follows the reader's device. Swap it for theme-light or theme-dark to pick one yourself (see Light, Dark and Auto Themes).

6.2.1 Header

The header goes first. Like the real app, it has a back arrow, the contact's picture, their name with a status line underneath, and the video and menu icons. It's a mob-header paragraph, the same as in every chat app, with each part in a span of its own:

← L Lily - online 🎥⋮ 

<p class="mob-header">
  <span class="back">
    <span class="ic ic-back">&#8592;</span>
  </span>
  <span class="avatar colour2">L</span>
  <span class="chat-name">Lily</span><span class="hide"> - </span>
  <span>online</span>
  <span class="btns"><span class="ic ic-video">&#127909;</span><span class="ic ic-menu">&#8942;</span></span>
</p>

The status line is the plain span after the name. It can say "online", "last seen today at 9:41" or "typing...". After the header comes <div class="mob-body">, which holds all of the messages.

Options: leaving the picture off

Leaving the picture off: if you don't want a picture, delete the avatar line, and the name moves up next to the back arrow:

  <span class="btns"><span class="ic ic-video">&#127909;</span><span class="ic ic-menu">&#8942;</span></span>
</p>
Options: a character with a profile

A character with a profile: put their profile in the circle and a name span in chat-name, with the name typed inside it in a hide span for readers with the workskin off. Then both come from the profile, they're the same in every chat, and they change to whatever your point-of-view character has them saved as (see Their Phone):

  <span class="back">
    <span class="ic ic-back">&#8592;</span>

    <span class="pfp pfp-shane"></span>
  </span>
  <span class="chat-name"><span class="name pfp-shane"><span class="hide">Shane</span></span></span><span class="hide"> - </span>
  <span>online</span>
  <span class="btns"><span class="ic ic-video">&#127909;</span><span class="ic ic-menu">&#8942;</span></span>
</p>
Options: a plain header with no picture or icons

For a quick chat, a single mob-header paragraph makes a plain bar (green in light mode, dark grey in dark mode) with just the name and status, by leaving out everything else:

Lily - online

<p class="mob-header">Lily<span class="hide"> - </span><span>online</span></p>

The name can be a name span here too, like <span class="name pfp-ilya"><span class="hide">Ilya</span></span>.

How it works: the parts of the header

The parts go in this order: the back arrow (back), the picture (avatar), the name (chat-name), the status line (a plain span) and the icons (btns). Leave out any you don't want. The hidden " - " after the name keeps the name and the status apart for readers with the workskin off. If the name or the status is too long, the workskin cuts it short with "..." for you.

The picture is the same avatar circle as in group messages, so it shows initials, and colour1 to colour10 work here too. An uploaded picture works as well, in a pfp span (see Uploading a Profile Pic).

6.3 Normal Messages

6.3.1 Simple Messages

WhatsApp bubbles hold more than one line of text - each one can have a time, ticks and a quoted reply in it. So instead of a single <p>, each message is a <div> with paragraphs inside:

Lily: are you still up?

23:41

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p><span class="hide"><em>Lily:</em> </span>are you still up?</p>
  <p class="time">23:41</p>
</div>

Same as iMessage, msg in is a white bubble on the left (dark grey in dark mode) and msg out is a green bubble on the right (dark green in dark mode). The first paragraph is the message itself, and time is the little grey time in the bottom right of the bubble. tail is optional - it adds the little pointy corner to the bubble, and it's meant for the first bubble in a run from one person.

How it works: each part of a message, piece by piece
  • <div class="msg in tail"> - the bubble, with everything inside it.
  • <span class="hide"><em>Lily:</em> </span> - only for readers with the workskin off. It goes at the start of the first paragraph, straight before the message. With the workskin on, the colour and side of the bubble show who's talking. With it off, they don't - and in a one-to-one chat there's no name shown anywhere else - so this label is how those readers know who sent it.
  • are you still up? - the message itself.
  • <p class="time">23:41</p> - the time, which shows either way.

With the workskin off, this reads:

Lily: are you still up?

23:41

Swap the name for whoever is talking. There's more on this in Accessibility.

6.6 Characters

6.6.1 Names in Groups

In a group, add a who paragraph as the first line of a bubble to show who sent it. WhatsApp gives everyone their own name colour, so add one of colour1 to colour10 as well. Use the same number as the person's picture circle, so their name and picture match:

Hayden:

should I bring the kids?

09:13

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p class="who colour4">Hayden<span class="hide">:</span></p>
  <p>should I bring the kids?</p>
  <p class="time">09:13</p>
</div>

Give each person the same colour class every time, so their name stays the same colour all the way through the chat.

Options: a name from a profile

For a character with a profile, add their profile name to the who line instead of a colour class, and their name colour comes from their profile, the same in every chat. The name itself can come from the profile too, in a name span with the name typed inside it in a hide span, or you can just type it (see Character Profiles):

Ilya:

I will bring the kids. I am very good with kids

09:14

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p class="who pfp-ilya">
    <span class="name pfp-ilya"><span class="hide">Ilya</span></span><span class="hide">:</span>
  </p>
  <p>I will bring the kids. I am very good with kids</p>
  <p class="time">09:14</p>
</div>
How it works: the hidden colon

The who name shows at the top of the bubble whether the workskin is on or off, so it doesn't need a hidden label - just the hidden colon after it (<span class="hide">:</span>), so it reads "Hayden:" followed by the message. Your own messages have no who line, so they keep the full hidden label.

Names that are too long

A long name over a message in a group goes onto a second line when it doesn't fit in the bubble. If you'd rather it stayed on one line, type a shorter name, or a nickname (see Phone Numbers and Nicknames).

6.6.2 Phone Numbers and Nicknames

In a group, anyone who isn't saved in your contacts shows up as a phone number, or as the nickname they've given themselves, which starts with a ~. WhatsApp puts the number at the right-hand end of the name line. To do that, put a wa-no span inside the who paragraph:

~ Rose +353 85 123 4567:

DINNER PARTY. my place, saturday at 7 🥂

09:12

<div class="msg in tail">
  <p class="who colour1">~ Rose<span class="hide"> </span><span class="wa-no">+353 85 123 4567</span><span class="hide">:</span></p>
  <p>DINNER PARTY. my place, saturday at 7 🥂</p>
  <p class="time">09:12</p>
</div>

For someone who is saved in your contacts, just write their name and leave the number out. If you want a name in the normal text colour (for yourself, for example), leave the colour class off.
