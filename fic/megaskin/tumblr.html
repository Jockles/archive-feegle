The Tumblr module is styled after the Tumblr mobile app: each post is a rounded card, and a reblog chain is a list of comments with a thin line between each one.

There are two kinds of thing you can build: a single post (here, a reblog chain), or several posts together on the feed, like scrolling the app. The full HTML is under each one, and the rest of the chapter takes it apart piece by piece.

  • tb-header is the header row at the top of the post.

14.4 The Header: Picture, Name and Time

The header has the blog's picture, the name with a small grey line under it, and the three dots. The picture is tb-pfp: with no image it shows initials, and colour1 to colour10 give it a colour:

RR ••• rinkrat 2h ago 

<p class="tb-header">
  <span class="tb-pfp colour2">RR</span>
  <span class="tb-menu">&#8226;&#8226;&#8226;</span>
  <span class="tb-name"><b>rinkrat</b>
    <span class="tb-subline">2h ago</span></span>
</p>

tb-name holds the name in <b>, tb-subline is the grey line under it, and tb-menu is the three dots. Keep them in this order: picture, dots, then name.

Why the dots come before the name

Anything on the right-hand side, like the dots or a Follow button, has to come before the name in the code, so the name fits in beside it.

For a real picture, put the picture tag inside a pfp span inside the tb-pfp. The green reblog badge (next section) works with it too:

<span class="tb-pfp"><span class="pfp"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Puck.png" alt="Profile picture of zamboni-zone, a hockey puck"></span></span>

Or use a profile, so the same person has the same picture everywhere, like Rose's quote post on the feed. The blog name can come from the profile too - type it inside a hide span as well:

••• PoseyRosey 1h ago 

<p class="tb-header">
  <span class="tb-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-rose"></span></span>
  <span class="tb-menu">&#8226;&#8226;&#8226;</span>
  <span class="tb-name"><b><span class="handle pfp-rose"><span class="hide">PoseyRosey</span></span></b>

More about profiles and colours

That's for readers with the workskin switched off. The blog name comes from the CSS, so without it they wouldn't know who posted (see Character Appearances and Profiles and Accessibility).

The colours for colour1 to colour10 are set in the core CSS and shared with every app, so you can change them there.

14.5 Round or Square Pictures

Pictures are circles unless you say otherwise. On Tumblr, everyone picks a circle or a rounded square, and it's the same everywhere their blog turns up.

For a blog without a profile, add sq to its picture every time that blog appears. It works on tb-pfp and tb-ask-pfp:

14.7 Verified Ticks and the Follow Button

Some blogs have coloured checkmarks after their names. On Tumblr these are bought for fun, so they don't mean a blog is verified - give them to any blog you like. Put a tb-checks span straight after the name, with no space, and ic-checks-2, ic-checks-4, ic-checks-6, ic-checks-8, ic-checks-10 or ic-checks-12 for how many, or ic-checks-blue for two blue ones. Type the checkmarks inside it too, for readers with the workskin switched off: <span class="tb-checks ic ic-checks-6">&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;</span>.

There's one in the Follow button example below, and samthecat has the two blue ones in Uploading a Profile Pic.

More about the checkmarks

They come in pairs, up to twelve, and always in the same colour order, round the rainbow: blue and green, lime and yellow, amber and orange, dark orange and red, pink and magenta, then purple and violet. A blog with lots of checkmarks gets them cut off where the Follow button or the three dots start, like in the app, so on a narrow screen you won't see all twelve. Tumblr has lots of other badges too, but the skin only does the checkmarks.

Checkmarks drawn with CSS instead of pictures

There's also a version drawn with CSS. It looks a little simpler than Tumblr's, but it doesn't need any pictures: add a tb-check span for each checkmark, like <span class="tb-check">✓</span><span class="tb-check">✓</span>, and the colours are added for you in the right order. For two blue ones, add blue to the second: <span class="tb-check blue">.

For a character with a profile, put the checkmarks in the profile instead, and they show up every time you use that blog. Luca's main blog has two and his art blog has six:

14.12 Quote Posts

Once a vote is in, it can't be taken back, the same as Tumblr. Refreshing the page resets it.

Each option's results are a separate copy, so they don't have to match. You can give each one its own bars, percentages, winner and vote count. That means whatever the reader picks can always win, or always lose, or end in a draw, or anything else you like. Just change the bar sizes, tb-pct numbers and win in that option's tb-results.

For a finished poll that your pov character voted on while it was live, put a tb-voter span on their option in the results too.

More about polls

The bar sizes go up in tens, with p5, p25, p33, p67 and p75 as well - pick the nearest one to your percentage. The bars don't have to add up, so nobody will check your maths. Every option is chosen by whoever made the poll, and that includes a joke "See Results" option like rinkrat's - it's just another option.

To show the results while a poll is still running, as someone who's already voted sees it, leave out win and put the time left in the tb-pollinfo line instead: 4,812 votes &middot; Remaining time: 2 days 4 hours.

14.15 Pictures and Gifs

Pictures and gifs go in a tb-photos box inside tb-body. Each row is a tb-row, and each picture goes in a tb-photo span. Don't forget the alt text (see Accessibility).

One picture on its own fills the width and keeps its own shape:

<div class="tb-photos">
  <p class="tb-row"><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat2.jpg" alt="Black and white cat screaming at the camera, on a red patterned rug"></span></p>
</div>

Add two to the row for two pictures side by side. If a picture is wider than it is tall, add class="land" to its <img>:

<div class="tb-photos">
  <p class="tb-row two"><span class="tb-photo"><img class="land" src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat3.jpg" alt="Black and white cat holding out a paw daintily, on a beige and green cat tree"></span><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat4.jpg" alt="Black and white cat sitting in an opened red suitcase, on a red patterned rug"></span></p>

Text and pictures can go in any order, as many times as you like, like Luca's post on the feed. A picture can go in a reblog too: put the tb-photos box in its tb-reblog, after the tb-blog header.

More about pictures

Pictures go edge to edge across the card, the same as the app, and a gif works exactly the same way as a picture. Two side by side are cropped to two squares the same size, so they always line up - land crops the sides of a wide picture instead of the top and bottom. Real photosets come in loads of layouts, and the skin doesn't try to copy them all.

Pictures at the very start of a post sit right up against the header, like the app. A plain <img> straight in the body, without the tb-photos box, still works too - it sits inside the card with a small gap round it instead of going edge to edge.

14.16 Mentioning a Blog

Put a mentioned blog's name, with its @, in a tb-at span: <span class="tb-at">@oghollanovtruther</span>. It turns a slightly different colour, like Tumblr, and works anywhere in a post, a reblog or an ask.

How the screen scrolls

The bar at the top scrolls away with the posts, and the buttons at the bottom stay put. The posts inside the screen don't scroll on their own - the whole screen scrolls instead.

To put posts on the feed background without the rest of the screen, wrap them in <div class="tb-feed theme-auto"> and </div>. Give the feed the same theme as its posts: theme-auto is navy, and black when the reader's device is in dark mode, and theme-dark is always black.

Other feed colours

Add black for black all the time, <div class="tb-feed black">, or classic for the old Tumblr blue, <div class="tb-feed classic">. You can change the navy to any colour in the CSS (see The Feed).

This is completely optional - without it, each post is just a card on its own, like the reblog chain.
