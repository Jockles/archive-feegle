The Tumblr module is styled after the Tumblr mobile app: each post is a rounded card, and a reblog chain is a list of comments with a thin line between each one.

Before we begin, here's the two kinds of thing you can build: a single post (here, a reblog chain), or several posts together on the feed background, like scrolling the app (here, an ask, a quote post and a chat post). The full HTML is underneath each, which you can copy and modify yourself. The rest of the chapter takes the HTML apart so that you can modify all the individual parts to say exactly what you want.

12.4 Tumblr Post Container

A Tumblr post has a header with the blog's picture and name, a body, and then the tags and notes along the bottom. The module class is tb. Here's the outline of a post, with the parts filled in with placeholders:

[the header goes here]

[the post goes here]

#hockey rpf

12,481 notes

<div class="mob tb theme-auto">
  <p class="tb-header">[the header goes here]</p>
  <div class="tb-body">
    <p>[the post goes here]</p>
  </div>
  <p class="tb-tags">#hockey rpf</p>
  <p class="tb-footer"><span class="tb-notes"><b>12,481</b> notes</span></p>
</div>
  • tb-header is the header row at the top of the post.
  • tb-body holds the post itself, written as ordinary paragraphs. You can put a normal <img> picture in here too, and it'll fit the width of the card.
  • tb-tags is the grey row of tags. Type the # in yourself, and use &nbsp; to space the tags out.
  • tb-footer is the row along the bottom, with the note count in it. You can leave it out, or add the buttons too (see Notes and Buttons Along the Bottom, further down).

Here are the tags from the reblog chain:

#cats  #the cat is right  #I love this website

<p class="tb-tags">#cats &nbsp;#the cat is right &nbsp;#I love this website</p>

12.5 The Header: Picture, Name and Time

The header has a round picture, then the name with a small grey line under it, and the three dots at the end. The picture is tb-pfp - with no image it shows initials on a grey circle, and colour1 to colour10 give it a colour:

RR ••• rinkrat 2h ago 

<p class="tb-header">
  <span class="tb-pfp colour2">RR</span>
  <span class="tb-menu">&#8226;&#8226;&#8226;</span>
  <span class="tb-name"><b>rinkrat</b>
    <span class="tb-subline">2h ago</span></span>
</p>

tb-name holds the name, in <b>, and tb-subline is the small grey line under it. tb-menu is the three dots on the right. The order matters here: picture, dots, then name. Anything that sits on the right-hand side, like the dots or a Follow button, has to come before the name in the code, so the name fits in beside it.

For a real picture, put the picture tag inside a pfp span inside the tb-pfp. The green reblog badge (next section) works with it too:

<span class="tb-pfp"><span class="pfp"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Puck.png" alt="Profile picture of zamboni-zone, a hockey puck"></span></span>

Or use a profile, so the same person has the same picture everywhere, like Rose's quote post on the feed. The blog name can come from the profile too, with the name typed inside in a hide span. That's for readers with the workskin switched off - the blog name comes from the CSS, so without it they wouldn't know who posted (see Character Appearances and Profiles and Accessibility):

••• PoseyRosey 1h ago 

<p class="tb-header">
  <span class="tb-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-rose"></span></span>
  <span class="tb-menu">&#8226;&#8226;&#8226;</span>
  <span class="tb-name"><b><span class="handle pfp-rose"><span class="hide">PoseyRosey</span></span></b>
    <span class="tb-subline">1h ago</span></span>
</p>

The colours for colour1 to colour10 are set in the core CSS and shared with every app, so you can change them there.

12.6 Round or Square Pictures

On Tumblr, everyone picks whether their picture shows as a circle or a square with rounded corners, and it's the same everywhere their blog turns up: the header, a reblog chain and next to their asks. The pictures in the skin are circles unless you say otherwise.

For a blog without a profile, add sq to its picture, and do it every time that blog appears so it stays the same. It works on tb-pfp and tb-ask-pfp, with initials, a colour or an uploaded picture:

PB ••• penalty-box-art 37m ago

PB penalty-box-art answered:

Anything you can eat with your gloves on.

<p class="tb-header">
  <span class="tb-pfp sq colour6">PB</span>
  <span class="tb-menu">&#8226;&#8226;&#8226;</span>
  <span class="tb-name"><b>penalty-box-art</b>
    <span class="tb-subline">37m ago</span></span>
</p>

<p class="tb-asker"><span class="tb-ask-pfp sq colour6">PB</span><span class="hide"> </span><b>penalty-box-art</b> answered:</p>

For a character with a profile, add one line to their profile instead, and their picture is square everywhere on Tumblr without adding anything to your HTML. Luca's art blog has one:

#workskin .tb .pfp.pfp-luca.acc-art { border-radius: 15%; }

••• gremlin-sketches 2h ago

new sketch of the cat. he would not sit still

Swap pfp-luca.acc-art for your character's profile (and their acc- name, if it's for another of their blogs). The line goes at the end of their profile.

12.7 The Green Reblog Badge

When a header belongs to a reblog, Tumblr puts a little green reblog symbol on the corner of the picture. To add it, put rb after tb-pfp. The grey line then usually says who it was reblogged from - write that name in <b> so it stands out:

PB ••• penalty-box-art Reblogged gremlin-sketches · 37m ago 

<p class="tb-header">
  <span class="tb-pfp rb sq colour6">PB</span>
  <span class="tb-menu">&#8226;&#8226;&#8226;</span>
  <span class="tb-name"><b>penalty-box-art</b>
    <span class="tb-subline">Reblogged <b>gremlin-sketches</b> &middot; 37m ago</span></span>
</p>

12.8 Verified Ticks and the Follow Button

Some blogs have coloured checkmarks after their names. On Tumblr these are bought for fun - lots of people have them as a joke - so they don't mean a blog is verified, and you can give them to any blog you like. They come in twos, fours or sixes, and always in the same colour order: blue and green, then lime and yellow, then amber and orange. So two checkmarks are always blue and green, and the other colours only show up on blogs with four or six. Add a tb-check span for each one, straight after the name, like <span class="tb-check">✓</span><span class="tb-check">✓</span>, and the colours are added for you in the right order. Tumblr has lots of other badges too, but the skin only does the checkmarks.

If a blog belongs to a character with a profile, the checkmarks can go in the profile instead, so they show up every time you use that blog without adding any spans. Luca's main blog has two this way, and his art blog has six:

••• gremlin-sketches 2h ago

new sketch of the cat. he would not sit still

#workskin .tb .handle.pfp-luca.acc-art::after { background-image: url(https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/15/tb-checks-6.png); padding-right: 6.2em; }

Swap pfp-luca.acc-art for your character's profile (and their acc- name, if it's for another of their blogs). There are three pictures, one for each number of checkmarks, and each has its own padding:

  • Two: https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/15/tb-checks-2.png with padding-right: 2.15em;
  • Four: https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/15/tb-checks-4.png with padding-right: 4.2em;
  • Six: https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/15/tb-checks-6.png with padding-right: 6.2em;

The line goes at the end of their profile. An extra account without its own line gets the main blog's checkmarks.

A Follow button is <span class="tb-follow"><span>Follow</span></span>. It goes after the three dots and before the name, which is why everything on the right-hand side comes first in the code below:

12.11 Tumblr's Default Pictures

When someone makes a new Tumblr blog and doesn't pick a picture, they get one of Tumblr's default ones: a white shape on a coloured background. I've put all five shapes on squidge, and they're see-through, so they sit on whatever colour circle you like. Put a colour class on the tb-pfp and the picture tag inside it, the same as any other uploaded picture:

12.12 Asks

An ask and its answer are two boxes, one after the other. The question box is tb-ask, and the answer box is tb-ask with tb-answer added. Each one starts with a tb-asker line, with the blog's name in <b> and then "asked:" or "answered:". The small picture on the right is tb-ask-pfp. It's a circle, or a rounded square if that blog's picture is square (see Round or Square Pictures):

B benchwarmer88 asked:

what's the best snack to bring to a game

PB penalty-box-art answered:

Anything you can eat with your gloves on.

So not nachos. Learn from my mistakes.

<div class="tb-ask">
  <p class="tb-asker"><span class="tb-ask-pfp colour3">B</span><span class="hide"> </span><b>benchwarmer88</b> asked:</p>
  <p>what's the best snack to bring to a game</p>
</div>
<div class="tb-ask tb-answer">
  <p class="tb-asker"><span class="tb-ask-pfp sq colour6">PB</span><span class="hide"> </span><b>penalty-box-art</b> answered:</p>
  <p>Anything you can eat with your gloves on.</p>
  <p>So not nachos. Learn from my mistakes.</p>
</div>

Those two use initials, with a colour class for the colour. It can also hold an uploaded picture, in a pfp span with alt text, or a picture from a profile, in an empty pfp span with the profile name. With a profile, the blog name can come from it too, in a handle span with the name typed inside it in a hide span:

samthecat asked:

what's your hockey hot take

alliknowishockey answered:

Bag skates are character building, actually.

<div class="tb-ask">
  <p class="tb-asker"><span class="tb-ask-pfp"><span class="pfp"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat2.jpg" alt="Profile picture of samthecat, a black and white cat screaming at the camera"></span></span><span class="hide"> </span><b>samthecat</b> asked:</p>
  <p>what's your hockey hot take</p>
</div>
<div class="tb-ask tb-answer">
  <p class="tb-asker"><span class="tb-ask-pfp"><span class="pfp pfp-shane"></span></span><span class="hide"> </span><b><span class="handle pfp-shane"><span class="hide">alliknowishockey</span></span></b> answered:</p>
  <p>Bag skates are character building, actually.</p>
</div>

For an anon ask, leave out the picture and write Anonymous as the name. Both boxes go inside tb-body, and you can put an <img> picture in the answer box after the text.

12.13 Quote Posts

A quote post is a big line of text in a serif font, with whoever said it underneath. The quotation marks are added for you. Put it in tb-body:

I go where I want.

— Scott Hunter, to a very brave reporter

<p class="tb-quote">I go where I want.</p>
<p class="tb-source">&mdash; Scott Hunter, to a very brave reporter</p>

tb-quote is the big quoted line, and tb-source is the smaller line with the source.

12.14 Chat Posts

A chat post is a block of dialogue in a typewriter font. Each line is its own paragraph, with the speaker's name in <b>:

Shane: why are you awake

Ilya: thinking

Shane: that's never good

<div class="tb-chat">
  <p><b>Shane:</b> why are you awake</p>
  <p><b>Ilya:</b> thinking</p>
  <p><b>Shane:</b> that's never good</p>
</div>

12.15 Notes and Buttons Along the Bottom

Like the real app, Tumblr shows one note count rather than a count for every button - notes are all the likes, reblogs and replies added together. The bottom row is tb-footer, and it goes after tb-body and the tags. The note count is a tb-notes span with the number in <b>, and it sits in a rounded box on the left. After it come the buttons, each a tb-icon span: save (tb-icon save, a bookmark drawn for you), share, reply, reblog and like, which line up on the right. Leave out any button you don't want:

4,217 notes ⇧ ✉ ⇄ ♡ 

<p class="tb-footer">
  <span class="tb-notes"><b>4,217</b> notes</span>
  <span class="tb-icon save"></span>
  <span class="tb-icon ic ic-share-up">&#8679;</span>
  <span class="tb-icon ic ic-comment">&#9993;</span>
  <span class="tb-icon ic ic-reblog">&#8644;</span>
  <span class="tb-icon ic ic-heart">&#9825;</span>
</p>

12.16 The Feed Background

In the Tumblr app, the white cards sit on a dark navy background (or black in dark mode). To get that, wrap one or more posts in a tb-feed, like the posts on the feed at the top of the chapter, and the cards sit on it with a gap between each one: <div class="tb-feed">, then your posts, then </div>. Add black for the black version: <div class="tb-feed black">.

This is completely optional - without it, each post is just a card on its own, like the reblog chain.
