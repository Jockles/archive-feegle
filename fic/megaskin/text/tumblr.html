Verified Ticks and the Follow Button

Some blogs have coloured checkmarks after their names. On Tumblr these are bought for fun - lots of people have them as a joke - so they don't mean a blog is verified, and you can give them to any blog you like. They come in twos, fours or sixes, and always in the same colour order: blue and green, then lime and yellow, then amber and orange. So two checkmarks are always blue and green, and the other colours only show up on blogs with four or six. Tumblr has lots of other badges too, but the skin only does the checkmarks.

The checkmarks are pictures of the whole set of two, four or six together, drawn to look like Tumblr's. For any blog, put a tb-checks span straight after the name, with no space in between, and ic-checks-2, ic-checks-4 or ic-checks-6 for how many. Type the checkmarks inside it too, for readers with the workskin switched off: <span class="tb-checks ic ic-checks-6">&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;&#10003;</span>. There's one in the Follow button example below.

There's also a version drawn with CSS instead of pictures. It looks a little simpler than Tumblr's, but it doesn't need any pictures: add a tb-check span for each checkmark, like <span class="tb-check">✓</span><span class="tb-check">✓</span>, and the colours are added for you in the right order.

Tumblr's Default Pictures

When someone makes a new Tumblr blog and doesn't pick a picture, they get one of Tumblr's default picture icons. I've put all five on squidge, and they're see-through, so you can give them whatever colour background you like. It's not exactly how Tumblr does it, but anyone who's spent time on Tumblr will recognise them. Put a colour class on the tb-pfp and the picture tag inside it, the same as any other uploaded picture:

Asks

An ask is a question in a grey box, with a tb-asker line at the top: the blog's name in <b>, then "asked:". The box is tb-ask, and it goes inside tb-body. The small picture on the right is tb-ask-pfp. It's a circle, or a rounded square if that blog's picture is square (see Round or Square Pictures).

How the answer looks depends on who posts it. When the blog that answered it posts it, only the question is in a box, and the answer is just the text of the post underneath it, with no picture. Put the tb-ask box first in tb-body, then the answer as ordinary paragraphs:

PB ••• penalty-box-art✓✓✓✓ 5h ago

B benchwarmer88 asked:

what's the best snack to bring to a game

Anything you can eat with your gloves on.

So not nachos. Learn from my mistakes.

The pictures work the same way as everywhere else. benchwarmer88 has initials, with a colour class for the colour. samthecat has an uploaded picture, in a pfp span with alt text, and Shane has a picture from his profile, in an empty pfp span with the profile name. Quote Posts

A quote post is a big line of text in a serif font, with whoever said it underneath. The quotation marks are added for you. Put it in tb-body:

I go where I want.

— Scott Hunter, to a very brave reporter

<p class="tb-quote">I go where I want.</p>
<p class="tb-source">&mdash; Scott Hunter, to a very brave reporter</p>

tb-quote is the big quoted line, and tb-source is the smaller line with the source.

Chat Posts

A chat post is a block of dialogue in a typewriter font. Each line is its own paragraph, with the speaker's name in <b>:

Shane: why are you awake

Ilya: thinking

Shane: that's never good

<div class="tb-chat">
  <p><b>Shane:</b> why are you awake</p>
  <p><b>Ilya:</b> thinking</p>
  <p><b>Shane:</b> that's never good</p>
</div>

Pictures and Gifs

Pictures and gifs in a post go edge to edge across the card, the same as the app. They go in a tb-photos box inside tb-body, and each row of pictures is a tb-row, with each picture in a tb-photo span. A gif is just a picture that moves, so it works exactly the same way. Don't forget the alt text (see Accessibility).

One picture on its own fills the width and keeps its own shape:

<div class="tb-photos">
  <p class="tb-row"><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat2.jpg" alt="Black and white cat screaming at the camera, on a red patterned rug"></span></p>
</div>

Add two to the row for two pictures side by side. They're cropped to two squares the same size, so they always line up, whatever shape the pictures are. A picture that's wider than tall needs class="land" on its <img> too, so the sides get cropped instead of the top and bottom:

<div class="tb-photos">
  <p class="tb-row two"><span class="tb-photo"><img class="land" src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat3.jpg" alt="Black and white cat holding out a paw daintily, on a beige and green cat tree"></span><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat4.jpg" alt="Black and white cat sitting in an opened red suitcase, on a red patterned rug"></span></p>
</div>

Real photosets come in loads of layouts, and the skin doesn't try to copy them all. Instead, stack rows on top of each other in the same tb-photos box, in any order, and you can make up whatever layout you like:

<div class="tb-photos">
  <p class="tb-row two"><span class="tb-photo"><img class="land" src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat3.jpg" alt="Black and white cat holding out a paw daintily, on a beige and green cat tree"></span><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat4.jpg" alt="Black and white cat sitting in an opened red suitcase, on a red patterned rug"></span></p>
  <p class="tb-row"><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat1.jpg" alt="Black and white cat staring through a gap between a screen and a desk, with blurred cables hanging down around and blue sky behind"></span></p>
  <p class="tb-row two"><span class="tb-photo"><img src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat2.jpg" alt="Black and white cat screaming at the camera, on a red patterned rug"></span><span class="tb-photo"><img class="land" src="https://images.squidge.org/images/2026/11/16/Cat3.jpg" alt="Black and white cat holding out a paw daintily, on a beige and green cat tree"></span></p>
</div>

Text and pictures can go in any order, as many times as you like: a paragraph, a tb-photos box, more paragraphs, another tb-photos box, and so on, like Luca's post on the feed at the top of the chapter. Pictures at the very start of a post sit right up against the header, the same as the app. A picture can go in a reblog too: put the tb-photos box in that tb-reblog, after its tb-blog header. A plain <img> straight in the body, without the tb-photos box, still works too - it sits inside the card with a small gap round it instead of going edge to edge.

Mentioning a Blog

When a post mentions another blog by name, Tumblr turns the name a slightly different colour to show it links to their blog. Put the name, with its @, in a tb-at span: <span class="tb-at">@oghollanovtruther</span>. It works anywhere in the text of a post, a reblog or an ask, and in light and dark posts.

Notes Along the Bottom

The bottom row is tb-footer, and it goes after tb-body and the tags. Like the mobile app, it shows the replies, reblogs and likes separately, each with its own count, and the share button on the far right. Each button and its count is a tb-stat span: the button first, then the number, then a hide span saying what the number is, for readers with the workskin switched off:

✉ 31 replies ⇄ 1,204 reblogs ♡ 2,982 likes ⇧ 

<p class="tb-footer">
  <span class="tb-stat"><span class="tb-icon ic ic-comment">&#9993;</span> 31<span class="hide"> replies</span></span>
  <span class="tb-stat"><span class="tb-icon ic ic-reblog">&#8644;</span> 1,204<span class="hide"> reblogs</span></span>
  <span class="tb-stat"><span class="tb-icon ic ic-heart">&#9825;</span> 2,982<span class="hide"> likes</span></span>
  <span class="tb-icon ic ic-share-up">&#8679;</span>
</p>

The replies, reblogs and likes always take a third of the row each, so they line up from post to post whatever the numbers are. If a post has no replies, Tumblr just shows the button, so leave out the number and its hide span: <span class="tb-stat"><span class="tb-icon ic ic-comment">&#9993;</span></span>. You can leave the share button out too, or the whole row.

The Feed Background

The feed at the top of the chapter is a whole phone screen, like the app: Tumblr's bar along the top with the Following, For you and Your tags tabs, the posts, and the buttons along the bottom. The bar at the top scrolls away with the posts, and the buttons at the bottom stay put. Add app to the feed, put a tb-screen box inside it holding the top bar and your posts, and put the tb-nav buttons after it:

<div class="tb-feed app">
  <div class="tb-screen">
    <p class="tb-top"><span class="tb-logo">t</span> <span class="tb-topicon ic ic-tb-shop">&#127978;</span> <span class="tb-topicon ic ic-tb-gem">&#128142;</span></p>
    <p class="tb-tabs"><span class="tb-tab on">Following</span> <span class="tb-tab">For you</span> <span class="tb-tab">Your tags</span> <span class="tb-topicon ic ic-tb-filter">&#8801;</span></p>

    [your posts go here]
  </div>
  <p class="tb-nav"><span class="on ic ic-tb-home">&#8962;</span> <span class="ic ic-tb-search">&#128269;</span> <span class="ic ic-tb-community">&#128101;</span> <span class="ic ic-tb-activity">&#9889;</span> <span class="ic ic-tb-profile">&#128100;</span></p>
</div>

Move on to a different tab to show a different one picked. The posts inside the screen don't scroll on their own - the whole screen scrolls instead.

In the Tumblr app, the white cards sit on a dark navy background (or black in dark mode). To get that, wrap one or more posts in a tb-feed, like the posts on the feed at the top of the chapter, and the cards sit on it with a gap between each one: <div class="tb-feed">, then your posts, then </div>. Add black for the black version, <div class="tb-feed black">, or classic for the old Tumblr blue, <div class="tb-feed classic">. The feed colour is separate from the posts' own light, dark or auto theme, so you can mix them however you like, and you can change it to any colour in the CSS (see The Feed).

This is completely optional - without it, each post is just a card on its own, like the reblog chain.
