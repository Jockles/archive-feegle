This chapter is Twitter before it became X, in two separate modules: twc12 for 2012 to 2015, and twc16 for 2016 to 2019. Each one has its own section below, and each works on its own, so add whichever fits when your fic is set, or both if it covers both. Neither needs the Twitter/X module.

10.1 2012 to 2015

Twitter in 2013: square pictures, stars instead of hearts, a bright blue bar along the top of the app and a black row of buttons along the bottom. Opened tweets had their words in a serif font, and the timeline in the app didn't show any buttons under the tweets at all. Here's a timeline, and an opened tweet with its replies. The full HTML is under each one, and the rest of this section takes them apart.

10.1.3 The 2012-15 App Screen

To show tweets the way they looked in the app, put them inside a twc12-feed app box, with an app-screen box inside it that holds the top bar and the tweets. The row of buttons goes after the app-screen, so it stays put. The tweets inside lose their own borders and sit one under the other with a line between them, and the screen scrolls once it's taller than a phone.

Options: showing every tweet at once

To show them all at once instead, add no-scroll to the twc12-feed.

10.1.3.1 Header

The blue bar along the top, twc-header, has the page name in twc-title, which always sits in the middle. On the home timeline there's a person button on the left, and on an opened tweet a back arrow instead. The search and write-a-tweet buttons go on the right. Each button is a twc-icon:

<p class="twc-header"><span class="twc-icon ic ic-tw-me">&#9786;</span><span class="twc-title">Home</span><span class="twc-icon ic ic-tw-search">&#128269;</span><span class="twc-icon ic ic-tw-compose">&#9998;</span></p>
<p class="twc-header"><span class="twc-icon ic ic-back">&#8592;</span><span class="twc-title">Tweet</span><span class="twc-icon ic ic-tw-search">&#128269;</span><span class="twc-icon ic ic-tw-compose">&#9998;</span></p>
10.1.3.2 Footer

The black row of buttons along the bottom, tw-nav, has Home, Connect, Discover and Me. Each one is a tw-tab with its icon and its name, and on lights up the page you're on:

<p class="tw-nav">
  <span class="tw-tab on"><span class="ic ic-tw-home-filled">&#8962;</span> Home</span>
  <span class="tw-tab"><span class="ic ic-tw-at">@</span> Connect</span>

</p>

Both bars stay put while the tweets scroll between them, and both are optional, so leave either out if you don't want it.

10.1.4 The 2012-15 Tweet

A tweet is a container with mob twc12. Inside, the name line goes in tw-top and the words in tw-text. The picture sits in a column of its own on the left, the name and handle go beside it, and the time goes over in the top right corner. Leave the dot off the time, since it doesn't sit next to the handle:

Stan @PuckStan 33m

Shane Hollander on the ice in practice today. The rookie looks fast #hockey


<p class="tw-top">
  <span class="tw-pfp">
    <span class="pfp pfp-stan"></span>
  </span><span class="hide"> </span>
  <span class="tw-name"><span class="name pfp-stan"><span class="hide">Stan</span></span></span>
  <span class="tw-handle"><span class="handle pfp-stan"><span class="hide">@PuckStan</span></span></span>
  <span class="tw-time">33m</span>
</p>
<p class="tw-text">Shane Hollander on the ice in practice today. The rookie looks fast <span class="tw-link">#hockey</span></p>

Twitter had no night mode in these years, so this module is always light, whatever theme you put on it.

Options: pictures, names, links and ticks

The picture in tw-pfp can be initials with a colour, a picture tag, or a profile, and the name and handle can be typed or come from a profile. Hashtags, @mentions and links go in tw-link to make them blue. A verified tick goes inside the tw-name, after the name: <span class="ic ic-verified">&#10004;</span>.

Options: names that are too long for the line

Any name can be too long for its line. When it doesn't fit, the handle shrinks first and can disappear completely, then the name is cut off, tick and all, and the time always stays. Put the name in a plain <span> inside tw-name and it ends in "...". Without the span it stops at the last whole word that fits. On an opened tweet, a long name wraps onto a second line instead.

Names that are too long

You don't need to do anything about long names: when the name and handle don't fit on the line, the handle is cut short first, then the name, and each one ends in "…", like the app did. Put the name in a plain <span> inside tw-name, the same as on Twitter/X.

How it works: each part of an opened tweet, piece by piece
  • tw-date - the date, written the way Twitter did: "8/2/13, 2:26 PM".
  • tw-counts - the retweets and favorites, with each number in bold. Twitter wrote the counts in capitals, and the CSS does that for you.
  • tw-stats - the buttons, with no numbers.
  • twc-reply-bar - the reply box, last of all.

In the app, the tweet being replied to sat above the opened tweet, and the replies were listed underneath it, all in the usual timeline size. You can see that in A 2012-15 Tweet and Its Replies, above.

10.1.6 2012-15 Retweets

A retweet showed the original tweet with a small grey line under the words saying who retweeted it. Add it after the tw-text:

<p class="tw-label"><span class="ic ic-tw-repost">&#8644;</span> Retweeted by Holster</p>

10.1.7 2012-15 Replies

There was no "Replying to" line back then: a reply was a tweet that started with the @handle of the person it answered, and replying to several people meant a string of @handles at the start. Put each one in a tw-link:

<p class="tw-text"><span class="tw-link">@irozanov</span> <span class="tw-link">@PuckStan</span> said the man who fell over at the blue line yesterday</p>
Options: the "Details" link

On the website, timeline tweets had a small grey "Details" link under them. To copy that, add <p class="tw-details">Details</p> after the words.

10.1.8 2012-15 Pictures

A picture goes under the words in a tw-photos, with the corners rounded to match the profile pictures: <p class="tw-photos"><img src="..." alt="..."></p>. One picture per tweet.

10.1.9 2012-15 Buttons

The buttons were reply (a curved arrow), retweet, favorite (a star) and three dots for more, all in grey with no numbers next to them. In the app they only showed up on an opened tweet, but you can add them to any tweet:

<div class="tw-stats">
  <p class="tw-stat">
    <span class="ic ic-tw-reply">&#8617;</span><span class="hide"> Reply</span>
  </p>
  <p class="tw-stat">
    <span class="ic ic-tw-repost">&#8644;</span><span class="hide"> Retweet</span>
  </p>
  <details class="tw-stat tw-tog">
    <summary><span class="ic ic-tw-like">&#9734;</span><span class="hide"> Favorite</span></summary>
  </details>
  <p class="tw-stat">
    <span class="ic ic-tw-share">&#8943;</span><span class="hide"> More</span>

The star is clickable: readers can tap it to favorite the tweet and it turns gold, and tap it again to take it back.

Options: buttons already used

For a favorite or a retweet the account has already made, add liked or reposted to that button's tw-stat, which makes it gold or green.

How it works: the hidden words

The hidden words are for readers with the workskin off, so the row reads "Reply Retweet Favorite More" instead of a row of symbols.

10.2.3 The 2016-19 App Screen

To show tweets the way they looked in the app, put them inside a twc16-feed app box, with an app-screen box inside it that holds the top bar and the tweets. The row of buttons goes after the app-screen, so it stays put. The tweets inside lose their own borders and sit one under the other with a line between them, and the screen scrolls once it's taller than a phone.

Options: showing every tweet at once

To show them all at once instead, add no-scroll to the twc16-feed.

10.2.3.1 Header

The bar along the top, twc-header, has the page name in twc-title, which always sits in the middle. On the home timeline the pov character's own picture goes on the left, in tw-me, and on an opened tweet a back arrow goes there instead, as a twc-icon:

<p class="twc-header"><span class="tw-me"><span class="pfp pfp-shane"></span></span><span class="twc-title">Home</span></p>
<p class="twc-header"><span class="twc-icon ic ic-back">&#8592;</span><span class="twc-title">Tweet</span></p>
10.2.3.2 Footer

The row of buttons along the bottom, tw-nav, is home, search, notifications and messages. Add on to the page you're on, and use the filled home button for the timeline. For a blue number on a button, like new notifications, put a tw-badge with the number in it straight after the icon. The circle stretches into a lozenge for longer numbers:

<p class="tw-nav">
  <span class="on ic ic-tw-home-filled">&#8962;</span>
  <span class="ic ic-tw-search">&#128269;</span>
  <span class="ic ic-tw-bell">&#128276;</span>
  <span class="tw-badge"><span class="hide"> (</span>3<span class="hide">)</span></span>
  <span class="ic ic-tw-mail">&#9993;</span>
</p>

Both bars stay put while the tweets scroll between them, and both are optional, so leave either out if you don't want it.

10.2.4 2016-19 Themes

theme-dark is Twitter's night mode, a dim dark blue rather than black, and theme-auto switches to it when the reader's device is in dark mode, the same as every other app. Put the theme on each tweet on its own, or on the twc16-feed for a whole screen.

Options: pictures, names, links and ticks

The picture in tw-pfp can be initials with a colour, a picture tag, or a profile, and the name and handle can be typed or come from a profile. Type the dot before the time yourself. Hashtags, @mentions and links go in tw-link to make them blue, and a verified tick goes inside the tw-name, after the name: <span class="ic ic-verified">&#10004;</span>.

Options: names that are too long for the line

Any name can be too long for its line. When it doesn't fit, the handle shrinks first and can disappear completely, then the name is cut off, tick and all, and the time always stays. Put the name in a plain <span> inside tw-name and it ends in "...". Without the span it stops at the last whole word that fits. On an opened tweet, a long name wraps onto a second line instead.

Names that are too long

You don't need to do anything about long names: when the name and handle don't fit on the line, the handle is cut short first, then the name, and each one ends in "…", like the app did. Put the name in a plain <span> inside tw-name, the same as on Twitter/X.

10.2.13 2016-19 Buttons

The row along the bottom of a tweet is reply, retweet, like and message, with the number after each icon. The like is clickable: readers can tap it to like the tweet, and it turns into a red heart and the number goes up by one. Tap it again to take it back.

Options: numbers, and buttons already used

Leave a number out if it's nought. For a like or a retweet the account has already made, add liked or reposted to that button's tw-stat, which makes it red or green:
