8.2.3 The X App Screen

To show tweets the way they look in the app, put them inside a tw-thread with app added, and a tw-scroll inside that. At the top go two lines that scroll away with the tweets: tw-apptop, with the pov character's own picture in tw-me and the X in tw-logo, then the row of tabs in tw-tabs. The picked tab gets on, which makes it dark (or white in dark mode) with a blue line under it. After For you and Following come any communities the character has joined, and the tw-add plus sign goes at the end:

X

For you Following Metros Fans Hockey Twitter +

8.2.5 Name and Handle

The first line, tw-top, has the account's picture, name, handle and the time:

  • tw-name is the bold display name.
  • tw-tick inside the name adds the blue verified tick. Leave it out if the account isn't verified.
  • tw-handle is the grey @handle, with the time after it.

The name and handle can be typed, like Scott's, or they can come from a character's profile. Put a name span in place of the name and a handle span in place of the @handle, each with a copy typed inside it in a hide span for readers with the workskin off. 8.2.10 Clickable Likes

Readers can click or tap a like to switch it on and off, like on the real app. It's made with the details and summary tags, like Instagram's heart, and goes in place of the plain like. Inside the summary are two counts: the one with off shows to begin with, and the one with on swaps in after a click, so the likes can go up by one. The heart and its count turn red too. Click again, and it goes back:

✉ 1 reply
⇄ 3 reposts
♡ 13 likes ( 14 once liked)
📊 351 views
🔖
⇧
<details class="tw-stat tw-tog"><summary><span class="ic ic-tw-like">&#9825;</span><span class="off"> 13</span><span class="hide"> likes (</span><span class="on"> 14</span><span class="hide"> once liked)</span></summary></details>
  • The hide spans are what readers see with the workskin switched off: "♡ 13 likes ( 14 once liked)".
  • To start with the like already switched on (a tweet the character has already liked, say), add open: <details class="tw-stat tw-tog" open>.

8.2.18 Polls

A poll is a set of tw-opt options, with a tw-pollinfo line for the number of votes and the time left. While a poll is still open, each option is just a blue outline. Polls work in a tw-reply as well as a tweet, like Stan's:

Montreal

Boston

179 votes · 13 minutes left

<p class="tw-opt">Montreal</p>
<p class="tw-opt">Boston</p>
<p class="tw-pollinfo">179 votes · 13 minutes left</p>

To show the results, add res to every option and put the option's words in a tw-bar span, which becomes the grey bar. Add a bar size from p0 to p100 to match its percentage. The sizes are p0, p10, p20, p25, p30, p33, p40, p50, p60, p67, p70, p75, p80, p90 and p100. When the poll has finished, the winning option also gets win, which makes its bar blue and its words bold. The percentage goes in a tw-pct span:

Montreal 75%

Boston 25%

97 votes · Final results

<p class="tw-opt res win p75"><span class="tw-bar">Montreal</span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="tw-pct">75%</span></p>
<p class="tw-opt res p25"><span class="tw-bar">Boston</span><span class="hide"> </span><span class="tw-pct">25%</span></p>

<p class="tw-media two"><span class="tw-photo"><span class="tw-desc">[A photo of the empty rink at night]</span></span><span class="tw-photo"><span class="tw-desc">[A photo of the same rink at 6am, full of children in helmets]</span></span></p>

All of this works in a tw-reply too.

8.2.21 A Scrollable Thread

A tweet with lots of replies can go in a scrolling window, with a "Tweet your reply" bar that stays at the bottom, like the thread at the top of this chapter. Wrap everything in tw-thread (with a theme class), put the tweet and the replies in a tw-scroll box, and finish with a tw-foot bar after it:

Reply - Tweet your reply

<p class="tw-foot"><span>Reply</span><span class="hide"> - </span>Tweet your reply</p>

The tweet and the replies keep their normal classes, but leave the theme class off them, because the tw-thread already has it. Only the box with the replies scrolls, once there's more than about a phone screen's worth. You don't need this for a short thread - ordinary tweets already scroll if they get too tall (see Scrolling Long Chats).

8.3.3 Twitter/X DM Window

DMs are built like iMessage - a container, a header and a body - with the module class twdm, so the container is <div class="mob twdm theme-auto">. First comes the header:

Shane Hollander - @ShaneHollanderHockeyPlayer

<p class="mob-head">Shane Hollander<span class="hide"> - </span><span>@ShaneHollanderHockeyPlayer</span></p>

The span in the header is the person's @handle. The name and handle can be typed, like Shane's here, or they can come from a profile, in a name span and a handle span with a hidden copy typed inside each. As in tweets, the @ is added for you, so only the hidden copy has one:

Zim - @ZimBits

<p class="mob-head"><span class="name pfp-zim"><span class="hide">Zim</span></span><span class="hide"> - </span><span><span class="handle pfp-zim"><span class="hide">@ZimBits</span></span></span></p>

After the header comes <div class="mob-body">, which holds all of the messages.

8.3.4 Simple Messages

Messages are paragraphs with msg and either in or out. Sent messages are blue and received ones are grey:

Shane: saw your tweet

Ilya: it was meant to be funny

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>saw your tweet</p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>it was meant to be funny</p>
Each part of a message, piece by piece
  • <p class="msg in"> - the bubble. in is grey on the left, out is blue on the right.
  • <span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span> - only for readers with the workskin off. It's hidden in the chat, where the colours and sides show who's talking. With the workskin off, they don't, and a one-to-one DM has no names shown anywhere else, so this label is how those readers know who sent it.
  • saw your tweet - the message itself.

With the workskin off, this reads:

Shane: saw your tweet

Ilya: it was meant to be funny

Swap the name for whoever is talking. There's more on this in Accessibility.

8.3.5 Timestamps

Twitter/X shows a small time under each message. Add it with twdm-time, straight after the message it belongs to:

Shane: it wasn't

10:14 PM

Ilya: it was a little bit funny

10:15 PM

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>it wasn't</p>
<p class="twdm-time">10:14 PM</p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>it was a little bit funny</p>
<p class="twdm-time time-out">10:15 PM</p>

The second time has two classes - time-out lines it up on the right, under one of your messages. Leave it off for the other person's messages, and the time sits on the left.

8.3.6 Reactions

To show a reaction under a message, add a twdm-reacts paragraph straight after it. Add out to put it on the right, under one of your own messages. The two hide spans are only for readers with the workskin off, so the reaction reads "[Reactions: 😏 1]" instead of a stray emoji:

Shane: ok it was a little bit funny

[Reactions: 😏 1]

Ilya: I am always funny

[Reactions: 🙄 1]

<p class="msg in"><span class="hide"><em>Shane:</em> </span>ok it was a little bit funny</p>
<p class="twdm-reacts"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>&#128527; 1<span class="hide">]</span></p>
<p class="msg out"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span>I am always funny</p>
<p class="twdm-reacts out"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>&#128580; 1<span class="hide">]</span></p>

8.3.8 Voice Messages

A voice message is a message with voice, holding a play button in twdm-play, an empty twdm-wave span (the CSS draws the sound bars) and the length in twdm-dur:

Ilya: ▶0:02

<p class="msg out voice"><span class="hide"><em>Ilya:</em> </span><span class="twdm-play ic ic-play">&#9654;</span><span class="twdm-wave"></span><span class="twdm-dur">0:02</span></p>

8.3.9 Start a Message Bar

To finish off the window with the "Start a message" bar, add a twdm-foot after the body, inside the container:

Start a message

<p class="twdm-foot"><span>Start a message</span></p>

The header and this bar stay where they are, and once the chat gets long, the messages in between scroll.

8.3.10 Group Chats

For a group DM, put the group's name in the header (and the number of people in the span). Above each person's messages, add their name with who. The name shows either way, so it only needs a hidden colon after it, not the full label - your own messages, with no name above them, still need that. A reaction can have more than one emoji in it:

Hayden:

are we still going out?

[Reactions: 👍 😂 3]

<p class="who">Hayden<span class="hide">:</span></p>
<p class="msg in">are we still going out?</p>
<p class="twdm-reacts"><span class="hide">[Reactions: </span>&#128077; &#128514; 3<span class="hide">]</span></p>

For a character with a profile, put a name span in the who line instead, with the name typed inside it in a hide span. DMs don't show profile pictures, so the name is all a profile adds here, but it keeps them the same as everywhere else:

Zim:

I'm driving, so yes

<p class="who"><span class="name pfp-zim"><span class="hide">Zim</span></span><span class="hide">:</span></p>
<p class="msg in">I'm driving, so yes</p>
